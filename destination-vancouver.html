Why Vancouver might be your place

Four reasons this came up at the top.

The only major North American city where you can ski in the morning and kayak in the afternoon — with a food scene, cultural energy, and outdoor access most cities simply cannot match.

⛷️

Mountains in your daily view

The North Shore mountains — Grouse, Cypress, Seymour — are visible from downtown and reachable in 30 minutes. Whistler Blackcomb, one of North America's great ski resorts, is two hours away. Stanley Park's seawall circles 1,000 acres of old-growth forest at the city's edge. Most cities get the city or the nature. Vancouver gets both.

🍜

The best Asian food outside Asia

Richmond (20 minutes south) is routinely called the best Chinese food city outside China by food writers who should know. Vancouver's Pacific Rim dining culture — Japanese ramen, Cantonese dim sum, Vietnamese pho, Korean BBQ — is world-class and reasonably priced. This is one of the defining daily pleasures of living here.

🌧️

Mild winters — with one honest caveat

Vancouver rarely freezes. Snow at sea level is uncommon. January highs hover around 7°C (45°F). But October through March brings persistent Pacific grey — not dramatic storms, but low clouds and drizzle that some people find cozy and others find depressing. This is the most important thing to test before committing. Visit in February, not July.

🏙️

A genuinely cosmopolitan city

Vancouver has the feel of an international city — diverse, design-conscious, professionally run, with a transit system that works. Neighborhoods like Kitsilano, Mount Pleasant, and Commercial Drive have distinct characters and real community feel. For retirees wanting urban energy without New York's intensity or LA's sprawl, Vancouver is a genuine alternative.

What a Wednesday in Vancouver feels like.

Morning jog on the seawall — the Lions Gate Bridge in the distance, kayakers already on the water, the mountains catching the first light above the treeline. You've lived here two years and the view still stops you mid-run. You always stop and take a photo.

Late morning dim sum in Richmond with the neighbors — har gow, turnip cake, the little egg tarts you always order two of. The conversation goes long and unhurried. Afternoon at the Vancouver Art Gallery, or a wander through Granville Island market picking up ingredients for dinner.

Home by 5, watching the sunset turn the North Shore mountains pink from the kitchen window. Dinner from the market haul — Pacific salmon, local vegetables, a BC Pinot Gris from the Okanagan. The city hums quietly outside. You're already looking forward to tomorrow's run.

The practical stuff

Three things to know before you go further.

Visa & Residency

No dedicated retirement visa

US citizens enter Canada visa-free for up to 6 months. There is no Canadian retirement visa — long-term residency requires Express Entry (Federal Skilled Worker program), a Provincial Nominee Program, or family sponsorship by a Canadian citizen or PR holder. These pathways are competitive and take 18–24+ months. Start early and work with a licensed Canadian immigration consultant (RCIC).

Healthcare

BC MSP — world-class, with a wait

BC's Medical Services Plan covers hospital and physician care at no monthly premium (premiums were eliminated in 2020). New BC residents face a 3-month waiting period — get private international health insurance to bridge this gap. Vancouver General and St. Paul's hospitals are exceptional. Family doctor shortages affect BC as they do most of Canada — factor this into your planning.

Taxes

Two tax systems, both real

BC's combined federal-provincial income tax is progressive and among the higher rates in Canada. As a US citizen living in Canada, you still file US federal taxes annually — the US-Canada tax treaty prevents most double taxation, but FBAR and FATCA reporting requirements on Canadian accounts carry serious penalties if missed. A cross-border CPA who specializes in US-Canada is not optional.

Victoria

Same Pacific climate and English ease at meaningfully lower costs and a quieter pace. A 90-minute ferry from Vancouver when you want city energy.

Atlantic coast, walkable old city, cosmopolitan culture at roughly half Vancouver's cost. A D7 visa is required, but the lifestyle per dollar is exceptional.
