A loose 3-day framework · Asheville, NC

What a good Asheville scout weekend looks like

Not a tourist trip — a reconnaissance. The goal is to figure out which part of Asheville is actually yours, and what everyday life would feel like in each one.

1
Friday — Arrive & orient
Downtown + River Arts District

Get in and walk the downtown core — Pack Square, Wall Street, the historic art deco hotels. Then drive five minutes west to the River Arts District for the afternoon: galleries, breweries, converted warehouse studios. This is the Asheville that gets all the press. Is it somewhere you'd actually want to be every day?

Downtown Asheville River Arts District Try: Biscuit Head for breakfast
2
Saturday — Neighborhoods
West Asheville vs. North Asheville

These two feel like different cities. West Asheville is eclectic, younger, more walkable — Haywood Road is lined with independent restaurants and coffee shops. North Asheville is quieter, tree-lined, closer to the Biltmore Village and UNCA — more settled, more traditional. Walk both neighborhoods before noon and decide which one fits your actual retirement.

West Asheville North Asheville Open house visit if possible
3
Saturday afternoon
Real estate reality check

Book at least one open house or contact a local agent for a showing. Not to buy — to calibrate. What does $450K buy in West Asheville vs. North Asheville vs. Black Mountain (20 minutes east)? Do a grocery run at the closest store to your favorite neighborhood. Price it against what you pay now.

Open houses Grocery shop Black Mountain worth a drive
3
Sunday — The important stuff
Blue Ridge Parkway + healthcare + the debrief

Morning: drive a stretch of the Blue Ridge Parkway. This is what a Tuesday in retirement could feel like. Afternoon: find Mission Hospital or an urgent care center — locate where you'd actually go for healthcare, and note how the drive felt. Then find a retiree meetup, a hiking club, or a neighborhood Facebook group. Spend the last hour writing down what surprised you — good and bad. That list is your real data from this weekend.

A loose 3-day framework · Sarasota, FL

What a good Sarasota scout weekend looks like

Not a tourist trip — a reconnaissance. Sarasota has multiple distinct personalities depending on where you land. The goal is to find out which one is yours.

1
Friday — Arrive & orient
Siesta Key Beach + downtown Sarasota

Get in and go to Siesta Key first — it consistently ranks among the best beaches in the US, and first impressions matter. Then head into downtown for dinner on Main Street. Walk the bayfront at dusk. This is the Sarasota that ends up in the brochures. Pay attention to how it feels when you imagine it as your Tuesday, not your vacation.

Siesta Key Downtown Sarasota Try: The Francis for dinner
2
Saturday — Neighborhoods
Lakewood Ranch vs. Downtown condos

These two lifestyle options are worlds apart. Lakewood Ranch (east of Sarasota) is master-planned, car-dependent, and immaculately kept — great golf, great amenities, active 55+ communities, very suburban. Downtown condos near the bayfront are urban, walkable to arts and dining, and significantly smaller per dollar. Walk both before making any assumptions.

Lakewood Ranch Downtown condos 55+ community tours available
3
Saturday afternoon
Real estate & cost reality check

Book a showing in whichever area interested you most. See what $400K buys downtown vs. in Palmer Ranch vs. in Lakewood Ranch — the differences are dramatic. While you're out, do a grocery run at Publix (ubiquitous in FL) and note prices vs. home. Also factor in: Florida has no state income tax and no estate tax. That changes the math for some people significantly.

Open houses No FL income tax Palmer Ranch worth seeing too
3
Sunday — The important stuff
Sarasota arts scene + healthcare + the debrief

Morning: Sarasota has a surprisingly rich arts scene for a city its size — the Ringling Museum, the Van Wezel Performing Arts Hall, independent galleries. If culture is part of your retirement vision, see how it holds up in person. Afternoon: locate Sarasota Memorial Hospital and note the drive from your preferred neighborhood. Hurricane preparedness is worth asking locals about directly — get real talk, not brochure talk. Then find a retiree group (the Sarasota Senior Friendship Centers are active) and ask three people what they wish they'd known. Write it all down before you leave.

Your scouting checklist

What to look for beyond the obvious

The scenery will impress you. These are the things that actually determine whether it works day-to-day.

🏘️

Neighborhoods & daily life

  • Can you walk to a café, a pharmacy, and a grocery store from your prospective street?
  • How does the neighborhood feel at 7am? At 9pm on a Tuesday?
  • What's the actual walkability — or will you be in the car for everything?
  • Is there noise: traffic, bars, neighbors? Visit on a weekend night, not just a weekday morning.
  • How far is the airport? How often do you plan to travel from here?
🏥

Healthcare access

  • Locate the nearest hospital and note the drive from your preferred neighborhood.
  • Look up Medicare Advantage plan options in that county — coverage varies significantly by location.
  • Are there specialists in your area of need within a reasonable drive?
  • If you're pre-Medicare age, check the ACA marketplace options for that state.
  • Ask locals: what's the actual wait time at primary care? Is it accepting new patients?
🏡

Real estate reality

  • Visit at least two open houses — not to buy, but to calibrate what your budget actually buys.
  • Ask the agent directly: how fast is inventory moving? Are prices still climbing?
  • Check HOA fees if considering a community — some run $500–$1,000/month on top of mortgage.
  • Ask about property taxes — they vary dramatically by county and can surprise you.
  • If renting first (highly recommended), check rental inventory in your target neighborhood.
👥

Community & social life

  • Find one retiree group, hiking club, or neighborhood community and show up — even briefly.
  • Are there people your age who've made this move recently? Talk to two or three of them.
  • What do people do on a rainy Tuesday afternoon in the off-season?
  • Is there a cultural scene, volunteer opportunities, or community organizations that match your interests?
  • Ask what they wish they'd known before moving here. That answer is gold.

Where to base yourself

Don't stay in a hotel. Stay like a resident.

Our recommendation

A rental house or apartment in the neighborhood you're most curious about

Book an apartment or house — not a hotel — in the neighborhood you actually want to evaluate. Cooking a meal, walking to the corner coffee shop twice, and wandering the surrounding streets at different times of day tells you more about whether you could live somewhere than any itinerary ever could.

What to budget: A decent apartment or house in Asheville typically runs $150–$250/night on Expedia. For a long weekend (Thu–Mon), that's roughly $600–$1,000 — call it part of the due diligence budget, not a vacation expense.
Tip: Book somewhere with a kitchen and at least two nights. The second morning — when you wake up without any agenda — is when you'll actually know how it feels to live there.
