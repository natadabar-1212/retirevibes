🇭🇷 Split, Croatia

Living inside a
Roman emperor's palace

Split is built directly inside Diocletian's Palace — a 4th-century Roman imperial complex where people have been living, working, and eating for 1,700 years. The Adriatic is steps away. Hvar is 45 minutes by catamaran. The city is walkable, warm, and genuine — and at $1,800–3,800/mo for a couple, it remains one of the most underrated places in Europe to retire.

Why People Choose Split

The vibe in four cards

Split is one of those cities that reveals itself slowly — the Riva promenade, the labyrinthine lanes inside the Palace, the island light at sunset. People come for a week and start looking for apartments.

🏛️
Diocletian's Palace — you live inside it
Diocletian's Palace is one of the best-preserved Roman monuments in the world and a UNESCO World Heritage Site — and people live inside it. The Palace walls contain a maze of limestone alleys, apartment buildings, bars, restaurants, and a cathedral (originally Diocletian's mausoleum, converted in the 7th century). Peristyle Square, the main atrium, hosts outdoor concerts and daily life. This is not a tourist zone that people leave at night — it's a living neighborhood with actual residents. The setting is genuinely unlike anywhere else in the world.
⛴️
Dozens of islands — by ferry
Split's ferry terminal sits at the edge of the Old Town and is the gateway to the Dalmatian Islands. Hvar — a UNESCO town of lavender fields, white wine, and extraordinary medieval streets — is 45 minutes by fast catamaran. Brač, home of the famous Zlatni Rat beach, is 50 minutes. Korčula, the birthplace of Marco Polo, is 2 hours. Vis — the furthest and most unspoiled — is 2.5 hours. Having dozens of inhabited island communities as weekend destinations, all accessible without a car or plane, is a quality-of-life variable that's impossible to replicate anywhere else.
🌊
The Adriatic — warm, clear, swimmable
The Adriatic Sea is warm (75–82°F in summer), clear to 30+ feet in depth, and swimmable from June through October. The coast around Split has concrete platforms, small coves, and pebbly beaches — Bačvice (famous for the local game picigin, played in shallow water) is 10 minutes from the Palace. Kaštela Bay to the west has quieter swimming spots. The sea is not just scenery here — it's the organizing principle of daily life from May through October.
✈️
Well-connected to the US and Europe
Split Airport (SPU) has expanded significantly — direct flights to London, Frankfurt, Amsterdam, Paris, and dozens of European cities operate in summer. For US connections, the easiest routing is via Frankfurt (Lufthansa), London (British Airways/United), or Amsterdam (KLM) — all under 2 hours from Split. Croatia joined the Schengen Area in January 2023, meaning free movement across most of Europe. For people who plan to travel widely or receive family visits, Split's connectivity is meaningfully better than it was five years ago.

Life on the Ground

A Tuesday on the Adriatic

You live in a 2BR apartment in Meje, a quiet residential neighborhood 15 minutes' walk from the Old Town — €1,100/month, sea view from the terrace, olive trees below the window. You moved from Boston 18 months ago. Your Croatian is coming along. Slowly.

Tuesday morning: walk along the Riva promenade to the Palace. Coffee at a cafe on Peristyle Square — the same square where a Roman emperor's mausoleum stands, now ringing with pigeons and the voices of people checking their phones. You've stopped being surprised by the absurdity of this. You order a second coffee.

Afternoon: a boat to Hvar on Wednesday means today is market day. The Pazar open-air market just outside the Palace's eastern gate has fresh produce, local cheeses, a woman who sells the best honey you've ever had. You spend €18. Dinner at home: grilled sea bass with olive oil and herbs, a glass of Pošip white wine from Korčula. €22 total for everything.

Evening walk along the Riva at 9pm — half of Split seems to be out, the sea glittering, children running. This is the evening passeggiata, the slow promenade that Mediterranean cities do and northern ones don't. You've come to understand why. After eighteen months here, you can't imagine living anywhere else.

Before You Go

The practical stuff

Croatia joined the EU in 2013 and the Schengen Area in 2023. The residency pathway for non-EU retirees is clear but has real income requirements.

🛂
Croatia D visa and temporary residence
Non-EU citizens (including Americans) can apply for a Croatia D (long-stay) visa and then convert to temporary residence. The income requirement for a couple is approximately €2,000/month in provable passive income (pensions, Social Security, investment income). Applications are processed at Croatian embassies/consulates in the US, then finalized with the local police department in Croatia. Temporary residence is typically granted for 1 year and is renewable. After 5 years of continuous residence, permanent residence is possible. Croatia does not have a specific "retirement visa" by name, but the temporary residence pathway functions similarly.
🏥
Healthcare in Split
KBC Split (Clinical Hospital Centre) is the main university hospital — a large, modern facility with specialist departments. Private clinics like Polyclinic Sunce handle routine and outpatient care more quickly for expats with private insurance. Most long-term expat residents purchase international health insurance ($3,000–6,000/year) that covers Croatia and EU travel. After obtaining Croatian residency, you can enroll in HZZO (Croatian public health insurance) — premiums are income-based but typically run €50–150/month for retirees. US Medicare does not cover international care.
☀️
Summer in Split — very busy
Split in July and August is overwhelmed with tourists — prices double, the Old Town is packed, and the city takes on a very different character. Many year-round residents escape to the islands or inland Croatia for peak summer weeks. This is not a dealbreaker — the other 9–10 months are wonderful — but it's worth understanding before committing. October through May is genuinely the best time to experience Split as a resident rather than a visitor. Spring (April–June) and fall (September–October) are exceptional: warm, uncrowded, and perfectly lit.
