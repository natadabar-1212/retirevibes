Las Terrenas,
reimagined.

A sun-soaked beach town on the Samaná Peninsula where French cafés meet palm-lined shores — and the lifestyle costs a fraction of anything comparable in the US.

🌊 Caribbean Beach ☀️ Warm Year-Round 🌍 International Expat Scene 🏖️ Samaná Peninsula
The Vibe

Europe meets the
Caribbean

Las Terrenas doesn't feel like most of the Dominican Republic. It feels like somewhere the world discovered quietly, loved deeply, and decided to stay. A sizable French and Italian expat community has shaped its restaurant scene, its slow rhythms, and its particular brand of effortless style — all set against some of the most dramatic beach scenery in the Caribbean.

This is a place to wake up to the sound of waves, eat lunch at a beachfront table, and spend your evenings watching the kind of sunsets that don't need a filter. It rewards people who want genuine community — neighbors who've made the same leap you're considering — without the manufactured feel of a resort town.

The Samaná Peninsula stays greener and lusher than the rest of the DR. Whale watching season in nearby Samaná Bay is a genuine spectacle. The beaches — El Portillo, Playa Bonita, Playa Las Terrenas itself — rank among the most beautiful in the hemisphere.

What You'll Love

Life in Las Terrenas

🌊

World-Class Beaches

Playa Bonita, El Portillo, and the town beach are consistently ranked among the Caribbean's best — uncrowded, warm, and genuinely beautiful year-round.

🍷

European Café Culture

French bakeries, Italian trattorias, and beachfront wine bars mix with local Dominican cuisine. The dining scene punches far above what you'd expect for a town this size.

🤝

Deep Expat Community

Thousands of Europeans and North Americans have put down roots here. Finding community doesn't require effort — there are clubs, events, and neighbors who've been through exactly what you're planning.

🐳

Natural Spectacles

Humpback whales migrate to Samaná Bay each winter in one of the world's best whale-watching events. Los Haitises National Park is a boat ride away. The peninsula stays lush and green all year.

🏄

Active Outdoor Life

Kitesurfing, snorkeling, hiking, horseback riding, and paddleboarding are all part of the everyday rhythm. The warm water and consistent winds make this a water sports hub.

✈️

Easy Access

El Catey International Airport (AZS) is 20 minutes away with direct flights from major US cities. Santo Domingo is 3 hours by road, with international medical care and all major services.

Where to Land

Neighborhoods & Areas

Las Terrenas has distinct pockets with different feels. Most expats settle within a short walk or drive of the beach — the town is small and navigable on foot or by motorbike.

Town Center

Most Social

The original expat hub, with the highest concentration of restaurants, bars, shops, and street life. Walkable to everything. Best for people who want to be in the middle of it. Noisier — especially on weekends — but the trade-off is community and convenience.

Playa Bonita

Most Scenic

A quieter stretch of coastline just west of town, with some of the most beautiful beach in the DR. More villa-style living, more space, more privacy. A short drive to town for shopping and restaurants. Popular with retirees who want the beauty without the bustle.

El Portillo Area

Most Relaxed

Further east along the coast — a long, wild beach with fewer crowds and a genuine away-from-it-all feeling. Good for those who want space, nature, and quiet as their default setting. Gated communities and standalone villas are most common here.

Pueblo de los Pescadores

Most Charming

The original fishing village tucked inside the town — wooden houses, colorful boats, and some of the best fresh seafood on the island. Walking distance to the beach. A romantic, local-feeling enclave within reach of all the expat amenities.

Know Before You Go

What to plan for

DR Residency Visa

The Dominican Republic offers a straightforward Pensionado (Retiree) visa for those receiving at least $1,500/month in pension or Social Security income. Permanent residency takes 1–2 years. A local immigration attorney makes the process significantly smoother.

Healthcare

Las Terrenas has basic clinics for everyday needs. For serious care, Santiago (2 hours) and Santo Domingo (3 hours) have modern private hospitals with English-speaking staff. International health insurance — around $200–400/month — is standard practice for expats here.

Currency & Banking

The Dominican Peso (DOP) is the official currency, but US dollars are widely accepted in expat areas and many landlords quote rents in USD. ATMs are available in town. Opening a local bank account is possible but can be paperwork-intensive.

Hurricane Season

The DR sits in the Caribbean hurricane belt. The Samaná Peninsula historically sees more rainfall and some hurricane activity, typically June–November. Many expats travel during peak season or carry wind/flood coverage. The rest of the year is largely clear and beautiful.

Language

Spanish is the official language. In Las Terrenas specifically, French is widely spoken given the expat community — English is functional but less universal than in USVI or other destinations. Learning basic Spanish pays dividends quickly.

Property & Renting

Foreigners can own property freely in the Dominican Republic. The real estate market is active — condos, villas, and apartments all exist at a range of price points. Renting first for 3–6 months before buying is the standard expat approach and strongly recommended.
