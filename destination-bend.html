Oregon has no sales tax. The mountains are not far.

Why People Choose Bend

The vibe in four cards

Bend attracts people who want to stay active well into retirement — and then discover, to their surprise, that it also has a genuinely good food scene, an arts community, and a pace that isn't as frantic as the outdoor reputation suggests.

⛷️
Mt. Bachelor — 22 miles from downtown
Mt. Bachelor is one of the largest ski resorts in the US — 4,318 skiable acres, 3,365 feet of vertical, and a season that typically runs November through late May. At 22 miles from downtown Bend, it's a true local mountain. Season passes are reasonable. Midweek in January, you can ski without crowds. For people who want skiing as a daily-life possibility rather than a destination trip, this changes the calculus entirely. The summit view — Broken Top, South Sister, Middle Sister, North Sister lined up across the horizon — is one of the best in the Pacific Northwest.
🚲
Trail systems from every neighborhood
The Phil's Trail Complex — 30+ miles of mountain bike singletrack accessible from the west side of town — is regularly ranked among the best in the country. The Deschutes River Trail runs 10 miles through the city. The Tumalo Trail connects to the Cascades. Paddling, fly fishing, rock climbing at Smith Rock (30 minutes north), and whitewater kayaking on the Lower Deschutes are all within the same day's radius. Bend's outdoor amenity set per square mile is among the highest of any small US city.
🍺
America's craft beer capital
Bend has more breweries per capita than almost any city in the country — Deschutes Brewery (one of the top 10 craft breweries in the US), 10 Barrel, GoodLife, Boneyard, and dozens of smaller taprooms are woven into the daily fabric. But this is a proxy for the broader food scene: Bend punches significantly above its size (population ~100,000) with James Beard-recognized chefs, a strong farm-to-table culture, and a restaurant density that reflects the influx of high-income remote workers and retirees over the last decade.
🌞
High Desert sun — not Pacific Northwest gray
The Cascades act as a rain shadow, keeping Bend dry and sunny while Portland and Eugene see 130+ rainy days per year. Bend gets roughly 300 sunny days — more than Miami. Summers are warm (highs in the 80s°F) and dry. Winters bring snow at altitude but the city itself can be clear and bright even in January. For people who assume "Oregon = gray and rainy," Bend is the correction. The high desert landscape — juniper scrub, volcanic rock, the Three Sisters glowing in the distance — is specific to this place and unlike anything on the west coast.

Life on the Ground

A Thursday in the Cascades

You live in a 3BR house in the NW District — $3,200/month, backs up to the Deschutes River Trail, two-car garage with a bike stand that's always full. You moved from Seattle three years ago. You gave up your parking spot for a ski pass and have never once regretted it.

Thursday: ski day. You're on the hill by 9am, midweek, and the runs on the west side are quiet. You ski until noon, eat a burger at the lodge, and are back in your car by 1pm. The drive home takes 25 minutes. You're showered and at your desk — you still do some consulting — by 2:30. This is a Thursday.

In summer, the same Thursday looks like this: up at 6am, mountain bike the Phil's Trail loop (2 hours), home, coffee on the deck watching the Three Sisters. Afternoon: drive to Smith Rock State Park. Scramble up the Misery Ridge trail. The view from the top of the Cascades is the same one you've seen 40 times. Still unreasonable.

Dinner: downtown Bend on Bond Street. A wood-fired spot with a good wine list and a chef who used to work in Portland. $72 for two with a bottle. You walk home along the river. There are worse retirements.

The Numbers

What it actually costs

Bend has gotten expensive — remote worker and retiree demand since 2020 pushed real estate prices up significantly. It's no longer a hidden affordable gem, but it remains cheaper than comparable Oregon cities with worse outdoor access.

Before You Go

The practical stuff

Oregon's tax structure has a few things worth knowing for retirees. The outdoor assets are genuine but have real costs.

💰
Oregon's tax picture
Oregon has no sales tax — a notable benefit for purchasing goods. However, it has a graduated state income tax ranging from 4.75% to 9.9%, and Social Security benefits are taxable in Oregon (unlike many retirement-friendly states). Pension income and retirement account distributions are also taxed. The tradeoff: no sales tax, no estate tax, strong public services. For high-income retirees, the income tax is a real factor. Oregon does offer a small retirement income credit for lower-income retirees.
🏥
Healthcare: St. Charles and beyond
St. Charles Bend is the regional medical hub — a 258-bed hospital with a Level II Trauma Center, cardiac services, and a growing specialty network. For complex procedures and subspecialties, Oregon Health & Science University (OHSU) in Portland is a 3-hour drive — a world-class academic medical center. Medicare accepted at both. The high-desert altitude (3,623 ft) means UV exposure is significantly higher than sea level — worth knowing for skin protection and outdoor activities in bright conditions.
📈
Real estate — understand the market
Bend's real estate market grew dramatically between 2019–2022, driven by remote worker migration. Prices have moderated but remain elevated. Median home prices hover around $600K–700K; rentals in desirable neighborhoods run $1,800–3,500+/month. The city has grown to ~110,000 people and continues to expand east and southeast. Choosing neighborhood carefully matters — the NW District and Old Bend neighborhoods offer walkability and trail access; the new developments further out require a car for everything and have less of the Bend character.
