Malta · Mediterranean Sea

Europe's smallest capital,
outsized in every way.

Valletta is a city carved from honey-colored limestone, packed into less than one square kilometer. A UNESCO World Heritage Site. English is an official language. And the Malta Retirement Programme offers some of the most favorable tax terms in Europe.

The vibe

The island where everything
is within walking distance.

Malta is one of the world's smallest countries — and Valletta, its capital, is the EU's smallest capital city. Yet somehow it contains more layers of history, culture, and charm per square meter than places ten times its size.


The archipelago sits in the central Mediterranean, 60 miles south of Sicily, and the light here is extraordinary — golden and warm, bouncing off millennia of pale limestone architecture. The Knights of Malta, who built much of Valletta in the 16th century, created a city of grand fortifications, baroque palaces, and gardens overlooking one of the world's great natural harbors.


What makes Malta genuinely exceptional for American retirees is the English language. Malta was a British colony until 1964 — English is an official language alongside Maltese, and virtually everyone speaks it fluently. No language barrier. No fumbling with translation apps. You can manage banks, doctors, lawyers, and daily life entirely in English from day one.


The expat community here is large, well-organized, and genuinely welcoming. Sliema and St. Julian's — the modern coastal towns across the harbor from Valletta — are the expat heartland. Valletta itself is more residential, quieter, and increasingly popular with those who want to actually live inside the UNESCO city rather than look at it from across the water. And if you crave even more peace, the island of Gozo — a 25-minute ferry from Malta — offers a slower, agricultural version of island life.

The Malta Retirement Programme (MRP) allows qualifying retirees to pay a flat 15% tax on foreign income received in Malta (minimum €7,500 tax annually). This is a major financial benefit that offsets Malta's slightly higher cost of living compared to Eastern Europe.

Where to live

The capital, the coast,
or the other island.

Malta's small size means every area is close to everything else — but each has a distinctly different atmosphere and lifestyle.

UNESCO heritage city
Valletta

The capital itself — a living UNESCO monument. Baroque palaces, fortification walls, extraordinary views of the Grand Harbour. Increasingly residential as the city gentrifies. For those who want to live inside European history, not just visit it.

Expat heartland
Sliema & St. Julian's

The modern coastal strip across the harbor from Valletta. Most expats land here first. Restaurants, cafés, the rocky Sliema seafront promenade (the Strand), gyms, supermarkets, and a full English-speaking social infrastructure for newcomers.

Peaceful sister island
Gozo

A 25-minute ferry from Malta, Gozo is dramatically quieter — farming villages, ancient temples, cliffs over the sea, and a pace that makes Malta feel rushed by comparison. Popular with those who want deep tranquility but close proximity to Malta's amenities when needed.

Day-to-day life

History you inhabit,
sea you swim in.

⚓
The Grand Harbour

The Grand Harbour is one of the world's great natural harbors, and watching it from a terrace at sunset — with the lights of the Three Cities glittering across the water — is one of those views that never gets ordinary. The harbor ferry between Valletta and Birgu (the old fortified city across the water) costs €1.50 and runs every 30 minutes.

🏊
The sea life

Malta doesn't have sandy beaches in the traditional sense — the coastline is mostly rocky limestone, with natural swimming holes, lagoons, and clear turquoise water. Comino's Blue Lagoon is among the most photographed spots in the Mediterranean. Scuba diving is excellent, with World War II wrecks at accessible depths. The sea is swimmable May through October comfortably.

🎭
Cultural life

Valletta punches well above its weight culturally. The Manoel Theatre (1731) is one of Europe's oldest working theaters. The National Museum of Archaeology houses extraordinary Neolithic artifacts from temples older than Stonehenge. Malta's annual arts festival transforms the city in summer. The opera season runs through winter with performances at the magnificent Teatro Massimo.

🏥
Healthcare

Malta has a universal public health system (free for residents) that is generally good for primary care. The private sector — Mater Dei private wing, St. James Hospital — offers shorter waits and English-speaking specialists throughout. Private health insurance is affordable at €100–200/month and recommended for expats who want full flexibility. No language barrier in any medical setting.

The practical side

What you need to know.

Visa
Malta Retirement Programme

The MRP grants residency to non-EU retirees who own or rent qualifying property in Malta and receive pension or investment income from outside Malta. Flat 15% tax on foreign income remitted to Malta (minimum €7,500/year). Straightforward application process.

Taxes
15% Flat Rate (MRP)

Under the MRP, qualifying foreign income taxed at a flat 15% in Malta. US citizens must still file US taxes and the US-Malta tax treaty applies. The combination of the flat Maltese rate and the US foreign tax credit can result in very favorable overall tax treatment — get specialist advice.

Getting Around
Bus + Ferry + Car

Malta has an extensive bus network that's cheap and covers the island well. A car isn't required in Sliema/Valletta but opens up the rest of Malta and makes life more convenient. Traffic on the island can be intense during peak hours. Driving is on the left (British legacy).

Property
Designated Areas Only

Non-EU citizens can only purchase property in designated Special Designated Areas (SDAs) — modern developments that are excellent quality but more limited in character. For more authentic property in traditional village cores, EU residency is needed. Most expats rent first, purchase later if at all.
