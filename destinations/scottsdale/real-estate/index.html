Know the city before you search

Scottsdale's neighborhoods,
honestly described

Scottsdale isn't one place. The right neighborhood depends on how you want to live — not just which one looks best in photos.

South Scottsdale & Arcadia

Simple & Comfortable

The more affordable and more local alternative — Arcadia in particular has become one of the most desirable neighborhoods in the Phoenix metro for buyers who want walkable restaurants, proximity to Camelback Mountain, and prices below the Old Town premium.

Est. rent: $1,400–$2,250/mo Buy from: ~$650K

North Scottsdale & DC Ranch

Comfortable with Extras

The premium resort communities in the northern desert — golf, gated communities, mountain views, and the highest prices in the metro. Where the amenity-driven retirement lifestyle that defines Scottsdale is most fully expressed. Convenient to nothing without a car but designed for people who have made that trade consciously.

Est. rent: $1,900–$2,900/mo Buy from: ~$650K

Old Town Scottsdale

Upscale

The walkable historic center — galleries, restaurants, the waterfront canal, and the most active nightlife in the city. The most in-demand address in Scottsdale and increasingly expensive. Where buyers who want to walk everywhere and accept the weekend tourist energy tend to look.

Est. rent: $2,150–$3,500/mo Buy from: ~$650K

The big decision

Rent first.
Almost always.

Whether you end up buying or renting long-term, starting with a rental gives you local knowledge that no amount of research can replicate.

Renting first

The lower-risk path to figuring out where you actually want to live

  • You discover which neighborhood genuinely fits your lifestyle — not just which one looked good in photos
  • You stay flexible while you figure out healthcare, social connections, and daily logistics
  • You avoid the costs of a quick sale if you change your mind in year one
  • You have time to understand local market conditions before making a purchase offer
  • In most markets, you can find furnished rentals for shorter initial commitments
Typical lease: 12-month minimum in most markets. Month-to-month options exist at a premium. Always read the full lease; tenant protections vary by state.

Buying when you're ready

Worth it when you have real conviction about the place

  • You've lived in the area for at least a year and you're confident in the neighborhood
  • You understand the local property tax, HOA landscape, and insurance requirements
  • You have pre-approval — lenders view retirees differently than W-2 earners; get this sorted early
  • You're not buying as an investment — you're buying because this is where you want to live
  • You have a local inspector and a buyer's agent (not shared with the seller) you trust
Budget 2–5% of purchase price for closing costs. Property taxes, HOA fees, and insurance are ongoing — factor them into your monthly cost calculation, not just the mortgage payment.

Before you commit

Three things to sort out before you move

🏥

Medicare stays with you

Moving within the US means your Medicare coverage moves with you. Medicare Part A and B are accepted nationwide. What changes: supplemental (Medigap) plans vary by state, and HMO/network plans may have narrow in-network areas. If you're on a Medicare Advantage plan, verify it has coverage in Scottsdale before you commit to anything.

📋

State income tax matters for retirement

Arizona's tax treatment of retirement income affects how far your money goes. Specifically: how the state treats Social Security, pension distributions, 401(k) withdrawals, and capital gains. Some states tax none of it; some tax all of it. Verify the current rules with a tax advisor before you finalize a move — the difference is material.

🏠

Property insurance and HOA costs

In the US, property insurance, flood insurance, and HOA fees are part of the real cost of ownership — and they vary significantly by location. Scottsdale may have specific exposure (wildfire, hurricane, flooding) that drives insurance costs above national averages. Get an insurance quote before you make an offer on a property, not after.

This is context, not legal or tax advice. Real estate, tax, and insurance rules vary by state and change over time. Verify current rules with a licensed professional in Scottsdale.
