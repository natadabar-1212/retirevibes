Why Medellín might be your place

Four reasons this came up at the top.

A city that completely reinvented itself — once infamous, now routinely listed among the world's most innovative and livable urban environments. And it costs half what Mexico does.

🌤️

72°F, every single day

The Aburrá Valley sits at 5,000 feet — high enough to tame the tropical heat, low enough to stay warm year-round. Medellín doesn't have seasons; it has two slightly different kinds of warm. Locals call it eternal spring, and they're not exaggerating. You'll put your winter coat in storage permanently.

🏙️

A city that earned its comeback

Medellín's transformation from the world's most dangerous city to a UNESCO Design City and tech hub is one of the most remarkable urban stories of the 21st century. World-class public infrastructure, a Metro system that runs on time, outdoor escalators that climb the hillside communities, and a cultural investment that has put the city on everyone's list.

💰

The lowest cost on this list

A comfortable couple's lifestyle — nice apartment in Laureles, eating out several times a week, private health insurance — runs well under $2,000/month. That's roughly half of San Miguel and a third of Lisbon. Social Security alone covers a comfortable life here for many retirees. The math is genuinely hard to argue with.

🏥

World-class healthcare at a fraction of US prices

Colombia ranks in the top 25 globally for healthcare quality. Medellín specifically is known for medical tourism — Clinica las Americas and Hospital Pablo Tobón Uribe are internationally accredited. A specialist visit runs $40–70. Private insurance for two adults runs around $150/month. The combination of quality and price is exceptional.

What a Thursday in Medellín feels like.

The Metro is clean and quiet at 9am, which still surprises you. You ride it down from Laureles to the Parque Berrio station, then walk through a neighborhood that smells like fresh arepas and cut flowers — there's always a flower stall here, because this is where Colombia's flower industry started.

Coffee at a café in El Centro that charges 80 cents and doesn't know what "oat milk" means. Then up to El Poblado for lunch — an international crowd at a rooftop restaurant, eating ceviche and drinking limonada de coco, watching the city unfold across the valley in every direction. The mountains are green and close.

Late afternoon: a neighborhood gym, then cooking at home with produce from the plaza market — mangoes, avocado, chicken, all of it inexplicably cheap and fresh. You video call your kids before dinner, and when they ask how you're doing, you realize you actually have a lot to say.

The practical stuff

Three things to know before you go further.

Visa

Pensionado visa

Colombia's Pensionado visa is built for retirees — if you receive a pension or Social Security income of approximately $1,350/month or more, you qualify. Apply at a Colombian consulate before arrival. It's valid for up to 3 years, renewable, and the path to permanent residency is 5 years of legal residence. One of the simpler retirement visa processes in Latin America.

Healthcare

World-class at ⅓ the price

Colombia's healthcare system ranks among the top 25 globally. Medellín specifically has internationally accredited hospitals (Clínica Las Américas, Hospital Pablo Tobón Uribe) that attract medical tourism from across the Americas. Private insurance through SURA or Sanitas runs $50–100/mo per person, covering specialists, hospitalization, and prescriptions. GP visits run $25–40 out of pocket.

Taxes

No US-Colombia tax treaty

The US and Colombia do not have a bilateral tax treaty, which means you need to be careful about double taxation. As a US citizen abroad, you still owe US taxes on your worldwide income. Colombia taxes residents on Colombia-source income. The interaction is manageable but requires a CPA who understands both systems — don't wing this one.
