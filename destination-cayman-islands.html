Why Cayman

The Caribbean
that actually works

Grand Cayman sits at the sweet spot of Caribbean retirement: genuinely stunning beaches, an English-speaking society, world-class banking infrastructure, and a stable political environment as a British Overseas Territory. There's no romanticizing the red tape here — there isn't much.

The trade-off is cost. The Cayman Islands is among the most expensive places in the Caribbean, and makes no apologies for it. Groceries, rent, dining — everything is priced above mainland US equivalents. What you get in return: Seven Mile Beach out your window, a tight expat community, and a pace that actually lets you exhale.

Daily Life

Your routine,
upgraded

Mornings start on the beach. Grand Cayman's Seven Mile Beach is consistently ranked among the world's best — and it never really crowds the way it does in tourist-heavy islands. You walk it before coffee. That's just Tuesday.

The expat social scene is active and welcoming. There are running clubs, dive clubs, sailing events, trivia nights, and volunteer communities. The island is small enough that you'll know people within a month. Shopping, modern restaurants, international grocery stores, and quality medical care are all on-island — this isn't a "fly to Miami for a dentist" situation.

Weather

Warm. Always.

The Cayman Islands sits in the western Caribbean, sheltered from many of the Atlantic's biggest systems. Temperatures run 78–90°F year-round, with the dry season (November through April) being the classic postcard version — blue skies, low humidity, light breezes off the water.

May through October brings the wet season: afternoon showers, higher humidity, and the possibility of tropical storms. Most residents simply adjust — indoor activities, afternoon naps, early-morning beach walks. Hurricane risk is real and worth factoring into your planning, though Grand Cayman hasn't taken a direct major hit since Ivan in 2004.

Seasons at a glance

NOV – APR · Dry Season
Peak weather. Low humidity, brilliant blue skies, light trade winds. Best time for snorkeling and diving. Also peak tourist season — expect more people on Seven Mile Beach.
MAY – OCT · Wet Season
Warmer and more humid. Afternoon showers are common but brief. Fewer tourists, lower short-term rental prices, and the island feels more local and relaxed.

Where to Live

Grand Cayman's neighborhoods

The island is small — you can drive it end-to-end in 40 minutes. But the vibe and price vary significantly by area.

Seven Mile Beach Corridor

The premium address. Beachfront condos, high-rises, restaurants, and resorts all along this famous stretch. Walking distance to dining and the beach — and priced accordingly. Most popular with expats who want everything on their doorstep.

Luxury · Walkable · Beach Access
George Town & South Sound

The capital area — more residential than resort, with local grocery stores, the main hospital, and a calmer feel. South Sound has a beloved park and running path along the water. More affordable than the Seven Mile corridor with better local character.

Residential · Practical · Good Value
East End & Bodden Town

The quieter, more laid-back side of the island. Fewer amenities, but genuinely beautiful and significantly more affordable. Popular with retirees who want space, nature, and a slower pace — and don't mind a 20-minute drive to George Town.

Quiet · Nature · Lower Cost

The Real Picture

What makes it special — and what to know

What works here
  • English is the official language — no language barrier, anywhere, ever
  • Zero local income tax, no capital gains tax, no inheritance tax — significant for retirees drawing from investments
  • Stable and safe — consistently ranked among the safest places in the Caribbean
  • Strong healthcare infrastructure with Cayman Islands Hospital and private clinics on-island
  • US, Canada, UK, and EU citizens can live here without a visa for extended periods — longer-term Residency by Investment options exist
  • Direct flights to Miami (1 hr), Atlanta, New York, Dallas, and Toronto — the US mainland is genuinely close
  • Active, welcoming expat community — meetups, clubs, charity events, sailing races, dive groups
  • High-speed internet and reliable infrastructure — works well for remote income or staying connected
Things to plan around
  • Cost of living is high by any measure — groceries, dining, and rent are all above US averages
  • Almost everything is imported, which keeps prices elevated and selection sometimes limited
  • No public transportation to speak of — a car or scooter is a practical necessity for most areas
  • Hurricane season (June–November) requires insurance planning and occasional evacuation readiness
  • Long-term residency requires a path: Residency by Investment (from ~$1.2M property), or ordinary residency after 8 years. Temporary stays are easy; permanent setup takes planning
  • US citizens are still subject to US taxes regardless of where they live — consult a cross-border tax advisor
  • The island is small — if you need variety, stimulation, or city energy, you may outgrow it
