Ancient island,

Paphos sits on Cyprus's southwestern coast, where Aphrodite supposedly rose from the sea. With 340 sunny days a year, English as a practical second language, and a well-established expat community, it's Mediterranean island retirement done right.

The vibe

Mediterranean island life,
in English.

Cyprus is the easternmost island in the Mediterranean, and Paphos is its most charming corner — a UNESCO World Heritage harbor town with ancient ruins, clear turquoise water, and a seamlessly integrated expat community.


Britain administered Cyprus until 1960, which left an enduring legacy: English is widely spoken, road signs are bilingual, the driving is on the left, and an enormous British expat community has been here for generations. For Americans, the practical ease of daily life in English is a genuine draw that more complicated European destinations can't match.


The pace is slow by design. Paphos isn't a big city — the greater area has about 70,000 people. There's no rush, no traffic, no sprawl. The waterfront promenade (the Poseidonos) runs along the harbor past restaurants, cafés, and the ancient Paphos Fort. The Archaeological Park, a UNESCO site containing extraordinary Roman floor mosaics, is essentially in the backyard of half the residential neighborhoods.


Winters are mild enough to sit outside for lunch in January. Summers are hot (July and August average 90°F+), but the entire island orients its life around the sea — the water is swimmable from April through November. The expat community here is among the most organized in Europe, with active clubs, social groups, and established support networks for new arrivals.

Where to live

Harbor, hills, or
somewhere quieter.

Paphos spreads from the ancient harbor up into the hills, with distinct areas that suit different retirement styles.

Waterfront & restaurants
Kato Paphos

The lower harbor district — restaurants, bars, the famous promenade, the ancient fort, and the Mosaics archaeological park. Most walkable area in Paphos; highest concentration of expats. Premium rents for the location.

Village life with views
Peyia

A village 10km north of Paphos, perched above the Coral Bay coastline. Stunning views, a genuine village square with tavernas, and a large established British expat community. Requires a car but noticeably more peaceful than the main town.

Residential & convenient
Universal / Chlorakas

Residential areas between Kato Paphos and the airport. More local feel, lower rents, proximity to schools and supermarkets. Popular with families and those who want a quieter base with easy access to the harbor.

Day-to-day life

Slow mornings,
warm evenings.

🌊
The sea is everything

Cyprus's water is famously clear and warm. Sea Caves north of Paphos offers spectacular swimming and snorkeling. Coral Bay is a wide sandy beach perfect for long summer afternoons. Scuba diving, boat trips, and paddleboarding are daily activities for many residents, not special occasions.

🏛️
History you can touch

The Paphos Archaeological Park — right in town — contains Roman floor mosaics of extraordinary beauty, dating to the 3rd and 4th centuries AD. The Tombs of the Kings (a UNESCO site) is carved from solid rock. Aphrodite's Rock, where legend says the goddess rose from the sea, is a 30-minute drive east. You are never far from something remarkable.

🍽️
Cypriot food culture

Cypriot cuisine is distinct — meze culture (small plates brought continuously), grilled halloumi, fresh seafood, slow-roasted lamb kleftiko, and local Commandaria wine (one of the world's oldest named wines, produced in Cyprus for 3,000 years). The taverna dinner — starting late, lasting hours — is the center of Cypriot social life.

🏥
Healthcare

Cyprus launched GESY (General Healthcare System) in 2020 — a universal healthcare system where residents contribute based on income and receive comprehensive care. Private healthcare is also excellent and affordable. The Paphos General Hospital handles emergencies; several private clinics serve the expat community with English-speaking staff.

The practical side

What you need to know.

Visa
Category F Retiree Visa

Cyprus's Category F visa is specifically designed for retirees with foreign income. Requires proof of €9,568+ annual income (individual) or €15,712 (couple), clean criminal record, and health insurance. Renewable every 3 years.

Taxes
Favorable

Cyprus offers a flat 5% tax rate on foreign pension income (after a €3,420 annual exemption) for non-domiciled residents — one of the most favorable regimes in Europe. US-Cyprus tax treaty exists but has limited scope; consult a specialist.

Driving
Left Side of Road

British legacy means driving on the left. US licenses are valid for visitors; a Cyprus license is required for residents. The roads are generally good quality; a car is practical for exploring the island and getting to hilltop villages.

Property
Active Market

Non-EU citizens can buy property in Cyprus with government approval (straightforward for residential property). Property prices have risen significantly since 2020 due to investor demand. Most expats rent for the first year before deciding whether to buy.
