Before you commit

The vibe quiz got you here. The scout trip makes it real.

You wouldn't buy a car without a test drive. Seven days in Porto is the test drive — and it's one of the better weeks you'll spend this decade.

🧭

The neighborhoods feel different on foot

Foz do Douro, Bonfim, Cedofeita — they read as names on a map. After three days of walking them, you'll know which one is yours. You can't get that from Zillow.

🤝

The expat community will tell you everything

Porto has a large, welcoming community of Americans and Brits who've already done what you're considering. They'll tell you what the relocation guides don't — the good stuff and the annoying stuff.

🔢

The numbers get real

It's one thing to read "~$3,200/mo upscale lifestyle." It's another to spend a week and actually track what you spend on coffee, dinners, groceries, taxis. Reality-check the estimate before you move the money.

A loose 7-day framework

What a good scout trip looks like

Not a tourist itinerary — a reconnaissance. The goal is to figure out where you'd actually live, not to hit every wine bar (though you'll hit a few).

1
Arrival day
Land, orient, do nothing important

Get in. Find your apartment. Walk to dinner — somewhere within ten minutes of where you're staying, just to see if you can. Notice how the streets feel at night. Sleep well.

Arrive & settle First impressions
2
Day two
Ribeira & Baixa — the historic heart

Walk the waterfront, climb to the Clérigos tower, duck into a pastelaria for a pastel de nata. This is tourist Porto. It's beautiful. You're here to see if you can imagine living near it, not in it.

Historic center Get your bearings Try: Mercado do Bolhão
3
Day three
Foz do Douro — where the expats live

Take the tram west along the river to where the Douro meets the Atlantic. Foz is leafy, calm, walkable, and where a lot of the American and British retiree community has settled. Have lunch at a café and eavesdrop. Find the nearest supermarket and do a real grocery shop.

Coastal neighborhood Expat-heavy Grocery shop here
4
Day four
Bonfim & Cedofeita — the local Porto

These two neighborhoods are where Portuenses actually live. Less polished, more real — independent bookshops, neighborhood tascas, families on the street. If Foz feels too quiet or too expat-bubble, this is your answer.

Local neighborhoods More affordable Try: Taberna do Largo
5
Day five
Healthcare day

Visit a private clinic or hospital (Hospital da Luz Porto or CUF Porto) and ask about expat registrations, private insurance plans, and SNS access for residents. Talk to a pharmacist — they're extraordinarily helpful and most speak English. This is the unsexy part of the trip and the most important one.

Healthcare research Book ahead if possible
6
Day six
Douro Valley day trip — lifestyle test

Rent a car or join a small tour and spend the day in the Douro. Terraced vineyards, quintas, wine at noon. This is what a Tuesday in retirement could look like. Notice how 90 minutes from your apartment feels like a different world entirely.

Day trip Wine country ~€40–80 for a tour
7
Final day — the real estate & community debrief
Walk apartments, meet the community, make your notes

If you haven't already, book a viewing of two or three apartments in the neighborhoods you liked most. Not to buy — just to understand what €250K or €1,100/mo actually gets you. Then find the local expat meetup (Porto Expats on Facebook is active) or a coffee morning, and talk to people who've done this. They'll answer every question you didn't think to ask. Spend the evening writing down what surprised you — good and bad. That list is worth more than any guide.

Your scouting checklist

What to look for beyond the obvious

The views will impress you. These are the things that actually determine whether it works.

🏘️

Neighborhoods

  • Can you walk to a café, a pharmacy, and a supermarket from your prospective street?
  • How does the neighborhood feel at 7am? At 9pm on a Tuesday?
  • Are there hills you'd need to navigate daily? (Porto has steep ones.)
  • Is public transit — bus or metro — within a 10-minute walk?
  • What's the noise situation? Street noise, bars, construction?
🏥

Healthcare

  • Locate your nearest private clinic and get a sense of wait times and English availability.
  • Ask at a pharmacy about prescription coverage for expat residents.
  • Find out the process for registering with the SNS (national health system) once you have residency.
  • Check if your prospective neighborhood has a health center (Centro de Saúde).
  • Understand the difference between SNS access and private insurance — most expats do both.
🌍

Expat & social life

  • Attend at least one expat event or coffee morning — even briefly.
  • Ask where English-language services are: churches, book clubs, running clubs.
  • Are there other people your age who've made this move? Talk to two or three of them.
  • Find out which neighborhoods the community gravitates to and why.
  • Ask what they wish they'd known before arriving.
📊

Real costs

  • Track what you actually spend each day — don't estimate, log it.
  • Price a week of groceries at a local market and at Continente or Pingo Doce (the main supermarkets).
  • Note what eating out costs at different levels: pastelaria, tasca, nice restaurant.
  • Get a real quote from 2–3 private health insurance providers.
  • Ask your accommodation host what utilities run per month.

Where to base yourself

Don't stay in a hotel. Stay like a resident.

Foz do Douro, Porto

Our recommendation

A furnished apartment in Foz or Bonfim

Book a full apartment — not a hotel room — for at least a week. Cooking a few meals, doing a grocery run, and walking to the same café two mornings in a row tells you more about whether you could live somewhere than any itinerary ever could.

What to budget: A good apartment in Foz or Cedofeita typically runs €80–€150/night on Booking.com. For a week, that's roughly €600–€1,100 total — call it part of the due diligence budget.
Tip: Search for "apartamento" or "apartment" rather than "hotel" to find self-catering options. Look for places with a kitchen, a washing machine, and ideally a balcony. Bonus if it's on a quiet street in the neighborhood you most want to try.

While you're planning

Two more things worth doing in parallel

Talk to a retirement advisor

The scouting trip tells you if Porto feels right. A retirement advisor tells you if the numbers work. Find a fiduciary who specializes in international retirement — they'll know Portugal's NHR tax regime, Social Security implications, and how to structure the move financially.
