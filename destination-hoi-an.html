Why Hội An

The world's most beautiful small town

Hội An has been called many things — the lantern town, the tailors' city, the ancient port that time forgot. What it actually is, for retirees who discover it, is something close to a dream: a walkable UNESCO-listed old town where the architecture is 16th century, the food is astonishing, the cost of living is startlingly low, and the pace of life is set to something between a slow walk and a bicycle.

The Ancient Town itself is car-free after a certain hour. You bicycle through yellow-walled merchant houses, cross the covered Japanese Bridge, stop at a bánh mì cart for $1.50, and watch the Thu Bồn River go by from a riverside café. In the evenings, the streets fill with silk lanterns and the smell of cao lầu noodles. It does not feel like the real world, and that's precisely the point.

An Bang Beach — one of Vietnam's best — is just 4km from the Ancient Town. Da Nang's international airport and full-service hospitals are 30km north. Hội An is small (under 120,000 people), but its geography is generous.

The Vibe

What life actually feels like

🏮

Living Inside a UNESCO Heritage Site

The Ancient Town is not a museum — it's a living, breathing neighborhood where residents hang laundry between 17th-century merchant houses and motorbikes navigate lanes barely wider than a bicycle. Living here means waking up inside one of the world's most beautifully preserved historic districts, every single day.

🍜

Hội An's Own Food Culture

Hội An doesn't just have Vietnamese food — it has its own dishes that exist nowhere else. Cao lầu noodles (made with water from a specific local well). White rose dumplings. Bánh mì that Food & Wine has called the best in the world. A bowl of anything here runs $1.50–4. Fine dining on the riverfront runs $15–25.

🎨

Artisan Culture & Creative Life

Hội An is Vietnam's tailoring capital — hundreds of skilled tailors will create bespoke clothing, silk ao dai, linen shirts, and leather goods in 24–48 hours. The town also has a vibrant community of painters, ceramic artists, and lantern makers. For retirees who want to make things — or be around people who do — Hội An has remarkable creative energy.

🚲

A Pace That Lets You Breathe

Life in Hội An moves differently. There's no rush. The town is small enough to walk, the weather is warm enough to bicycle, and the rhythm of daily life — morning market, midday nap, river walk at sunset — imposes its own gentle structure. For retirees leaving behind decades of urgency, this pace is, for many, the whole point.

A Day in the Life

Your Thursday in Hội An

6:30 am

Morning Market & Riverside Coffee

The Hội An Central Market is at its best before 8am — vendors arranging lotus flowers, fresh herbs, river fish, and jackfruit in elaborate pyramids. You pick up ingredients for dinner (roughly $3 total), then settle at a riverside café with a cà phê trứng (egg coffee) and watch the fishing boats move slowly on the Thu Bồn.

9:30 am

Tailors, Temple, & Ancient Town

A fitting at your tailor for the linen shirts you ordered two days ago. Then a wander through the Ancient Town — the Japanese Covered Bridge, the Fujian Assembly Hall, the rows of yellow merchant houses. No agenda. You've lived here long enough that tourists photograph you as part of the scenery.

3:00 pm

Beach & Lantern-Lit Dinner

A quick bicycle to An Bang Beach for a couple of hours — swim, a cold Saigon beer at a beachside shack, read. Back in town as dusk falls and the lanterns come on. Dinner at a riverside restaurant: white rose dumplings, grilled fish, local rice wine. The sky reflects orange in the river. You're home by 9.

Where to Live

Neighborhoods worth knowing

Most Atmospheric

Ancient Town

Living inside the UNESCO zone means walking out your door into lantern-lit lanes and 400-year-old architecture. Rentals here are boutique villas and traditional merchant house apartments. Pricier by local standards; still extraordinary value.

Beach Access

An Bang Beach Area

4km from the Ancient Town, An Bang has a laid-back beach community of expats, surfers, and café owners. Wide sandy beach, excellent sunset bars, quieter than the town center. Ideal for those who want beach access as a daily reality, not a day trip.

Local & Affordable

Cẩm Nam Island

A small island connected to the Ancient Town by a footbridge, Cẩm Nam is quieter, greener, and more residential. Popular with long-term expats who want proximity to the action without living in it. Excellent local restaurants, very little tourist traffic.

Healthcare

Local Clinics & Da Nang Hospitals

Hội An has the Hội An Family Medical Practice, a well-regarded clinic serving the expat community with English-speaking doctors and routine care. For anything more complex — surgery, imaging, specialist cardiology — Da Nang is 30 minutes north and has several well-equipped hospitals, including Da Nang Hospital and Hoan My Hospital.

Most Hội An expats carry international health insurance and treat Da Nang as their medical hub. Costs in Vietnam are dramatically lower than in the West even at the best private facilities — an MRI typically costs $80–150 USD.

Visas & Residency

E-Visa & Renewable Stay Options

Vietnam does not have a dedicated retirement visa. The most common path for long-stay expats is Vietnam's e-visa — now issued for 90 days with a single entry or multiple entries. Many retirees cycle through 90-day e-visas with brief border crossings (Laos, Cambodia, or Thailand) to reset. It's workable, but requires some planning.

Alternative routes include business visas, investor visas (for property purchase or business ownership), and — increasingly — longer-stay arrangements through sponsored accommodation. Vietnam's visa landscape is evolving; a local immigration specialist or expat community group (Hội An has active Facebook communities) is the best source of current guidance.
