🇬🇩 Grenada · Southern Caribbean

Grenada,
alive.

The Spice Isle. Lush rainforest tumbling to turquoise bays, the scent of nutmeg and cinnamon in the air, English-speaking and genuinely warm — and sitting below the hurricane belt where most storms pass north.

🌿 Lush & Green 🌀 Lower Hurricane Risk 🗣️ English Speaking 🏡 Citizenship by Investment
The Vibe

The Caribbean that
still feels real.

Grenada hasn't been overrun. It doesn't have cruise ship crowds stacking up at every beach, or resort corridors that feel interchangeable with anywhere else. It has a capital — St. George's — that many call the most beautiful in the Caribbean, with a horseshoe harbor, Georgian colonial buildings in colorful pastels, and hillside streets that reward wandering.

The island is deeply lush. Inland, you're in rainforest — waterfalls, spice plantations, birdsong. On the coast, you're at sheltered bays with calm, clear water. The combination of environments on a 21-mile-long island is quietly extraordinary.

Grenada sits at the southern end of the Caribbean chain, below the main hurricane track. It's not immune — Ivan hit directly in 2004 and caused serious damage — but the statistical risk is meaningfully lower than islands further north. For retirees who spent a lot of time thinking about hurricanes, that matters.

What You'll Love

Life in Grenada

🌿

Extraordinary Natural Beauty

Grenada packs rainforest, waterfalls, spice plantations, mangroves, and Caribbean beaches onto one 21-mile island. The interior is hiking and waterfall country. The coast is snorkeling and sailing country.

🌀

Lower Hurricane Risk

Sitting at 12°N latitude — south of the main hurricane corridor — Grenada sees significantly fewer direct hits than islands like Puerto Rico, USVI, or the Bahamas. It's not zero risk, but it's notably lower.

🗣️

English-Speaking Island

English is the official language — no language barrier to navigate on day one. Government offices, banks, hospitals, and neighbors all operate in English. The transition is easier than most Caribbean moves.

🏡

Citizenship by Investment

Grenada's CBI program — one of the Caribbean's most respected — offers citizenship through a qualifying investment. Grenada citizenship includes visa-free access to the UK, EU Schengen zone, and China. Worth exploring with an immigration attorney.

🎣

Sailing & Sea Life

Grenada is a major Caribbean sailing hub — the Grenada Sailing Festival is a highlight of the regional calendar. The Molinere Underwater Sculpture Park off the west coast is a unique snorkeling and diving experience.

🏥

Improving Healthcare

St. George's General Hospital handles most needs. Private clinics in the capital cover a good range of services. For complex procedures, Trinidad (45 min by plane) and Barbados have modern private hospitals with good reputations.

Where to Land

Areas & Communities

Grenada is small enough that location choices are about vibe more than distance — nowhere is more than an hour from anywhere else.

Grand Anse

Most Popular

The two-mile Grand Anse Beach is the island's crown jewel — calm, wide, lined with palms. The area around it has the most restaurants, supermarkets, and services. The main expat hub. Easy access to the capital St. George's, 10 minutes north.

Lance aux Épines

Most Established

The peninsula just south of Grand Anse — a long-established expat community with yacht club, marinas, and quiet residential roads. A mix of villas and homes. Well-regarded as one of the most comfortable and secure areas on the island.

St. George's

Most Vibrant

The capital is genuinely one of the Caribbean's most beautiful towns — horseshoe harbor, colorful Georgian buildings, a working waterfront with fishing boats and ferries. Urban by Grenadian standards. Good for people who want to be in the middle of local life.

Carriacou

Most Remote

A smaller island 23 miles north of Grenada proper — quieter, more local, lower cost, and ferry-accessible. A handful of expats have put down roots here for a genuinely off-the-beaten-track Caribbean life. Not for everyone, but unforgettable for the right person.

Know Before You Go

What to plan for

Residency Options

Grenada offers a Retirement Residency permit for those who can demonstrate sufficient income. The CBI program offers a faster route to permanent residency and citizenship through a qualifying real estate investment (from $220,000) or a national transformation fund contribution. An immigration attorney is valuable here.

Eastern Caribbean Dollar

Grenada uses the Eastern Caribbean Dollar (XCD), which has been pegged to the USD at 2.70 XCD to 1 USD for decades — one of the most stable exchange arrangements in the Caribbean. USD is widely accepted at the exchange rate. Simple banking picture.

Healthcare Planning

Day-to-day healthcare is manageable on the island. For anything serious — cardiac, oncology, complex surgery — planning ahead for medical travel to Trinidad or Barbados is wise. International health insurance ($200–400/month for a healthy retiree) with medical evacuation coverage is standard practice.

Hurricane Risk (Context)

Grenada is less exposed than northern Caribbean islands but not immune. Hurricane Ivan (2004) caused major damage. Most years pass without a direct hit. Long-term residents maintain hurricane shutters and carry wind insurance. Being south of the main track means more frequent near misses than direct hits historically.

Internet & Infrastructure

Reliable high-speed internet is available across most of the island — fiber connections reach Grand Anse and Lance aux Épines. Mobile coverage is good. Power can be inconsistent; a UPS or small generator is a common expat investment. The island is well-maintained and functional for day-to-day life.

Community & Culture

Grenadians are widely described as among the warmest and most welcoming people in the Caribbean. The expat community is smaller than Barbados or TCI — which some residents see as a feature, not a bug. Festivals, sailing events, and local culture create a rich social calendar year-round.
