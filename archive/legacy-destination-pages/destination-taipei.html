🇹🇼 Taipei, Taiwan

Night markets, world-class food, and a Gold Card visa with national health coverage

Taipei is one of Asia's most livable cities — compact, safe, clean, extraordinarily well-connected by public transit, and possessed of a food culture that rivals anywhere on earth. Taiwan's Gold Card visa offers a straightforward long-stay pathway for qualified professionals and retirees. National Health Insurance (NHI) — one of the world's most comprehensive single-payer systems — is available to legal residents after 6 months for approximately $35/month. Taipei is the city that surprises people most: they expect it to be like Japan or China, and it is entirely its own thing.

Why People Choose Taipei

The vibe in four cards

Taipei rewards people who engage with it rather than observe it. The food, the temples, the hiking, the night markets — all of it is more interesting up close and personal than at tourist distance.

🥟
The food — one of the world's great food cities
Taipei's food culture is arguably the finest in East Asia for density, range, and quality-to-cost ratio. The argument starts here: Din Tai Fung (originated in Taipei's Da'an district, now globally franchised) elevated the xiao long bao (soup dumpling) to fine dining standard; the original on Xinyi Road still operates. Night market food — scallion pancakes (cong you bing), oyster vermicelli (ô-á-mi-suànn), stinky tofu (chòudòufu), pork blood rice cake (tiě bǎn zhū xuě gāo), shaved ice with mango and condensed milk — represents an entire food culture that has no equivalent in mainland Chinese or Japanese cooking. Beef noodle soup (niúròu miàn) at Yong Kang Street restaurants is Taipei's signature dish; $4–8 a bowl, debated as seriously as Neapolitan pizza. Shilin Night Market (the largest), Raohe Street Night Market, and Tonghua Night Market each have distinct characters. For people who organize significant portions of their lives around food, Taipei is simply one of the best places on the planet to live.
🏯
Temples, traditional culture, and the Taiwanese character
Taiwan's unique historical position — under successive influences of indigenous Austronesian culture, Han Chinese settlement, Japanese colonial rule (1895–1945), and the Republic of China government — produced a cultural synthesis that is distinct from mainland China, Japan, and anywhere else. Taipei's Longshan Temple (1738, rebuilt 1957) is a functioning Taoist-Buddhist temple complex in Wanhua district where worshippers bring incense offerings daily; it is not a museum or a tourist site — it is a living religious institution. The National Palace Museum holds one of the world's greatest collections of Chinese art and artifacts (approximately 700,000 objects, brought to Taiwan in 1949). The Taiwanese concept of warmth and helpfulness to strangers (known broadly as rè qíng — "warm passion") is frequently cited by long-term foreign residents as one of the most distinctive things about living here. Taiwanese people are genuinely, consistently kind to foreigners in a way that is not performative or tourist-directed.
🚇
Infrastructure — among the best in the world
Taipei's infrastructure is exceptional by any global standard. The MRT (metro system) — 5 lines, 131 stations, 1-minute frequency at peak hours, universally air-conditioned — covers the entire city and is clean, safe, and on time. A single ride: NT$20–65 (~$0.60–2.00 USD). An EasyCard (contactless transit card) makes transit seamless. The YouBike bicycle-sharing system (2,700+ stations, NT$5 for first 30 minutes) supplements the MRT for last-mile travel. Internet: Taiwan has some of the fastest and most reliable broadband internet in the world — gigabit fiber is widely available for approximately $20–35/month. Healthcare: once enrolled in NHI (after 6 months legal residency), the system covers outpatient care, hospitalization, most surgeries, and prescriptions at nominal copays — a GP visit costs approximately NT$150 ($5 USD) with NHI coverage. This infrastructure baseline — transit, internet, healthcare — substantially changes the cost and quality of daily life in ways that are hard to quantify until you live here.
🏔️
Mountains and nature — forty minutes from downtown
Taipei is surrounded by mountains on three sides — the city sits in the Taipei Basin, ringed by the Datun, Qixing, and Nangang ranges. Yangmingshan National Park begins at the northern edge of the city — a volcanic mountain park with hiking trails, hot springs, and seasonal flower fields, accessible by city bus from Jiantan MRT station. The Seven Star Peak (Qixingshan, 1,120m) trail is a 3-4 hour round trip with 360-degree views over the city, the Taiwan Strait, and on clear days toward the Central Mountain Range. Maokong (茂公) gondola connects to the city's tea plantation district, where outdoor tea houses overlook the city below. Bitan (碧潭) riverside park is 30 minutes from downtown by MRT and provides a full river recreation zone. For people who need both city infrastructure and immediate access to mountains and nature, Taipei's geography is genuinely exceptional — the transit system puts you at a mountain trailhead in 40 minutes from the city center.

Life on the Ground

A Sunday between the mountain and the market

You rent a 2BR apartment in the Da'an district — NT$28,000/month (~$880 USD), 7th floor, across from Da'an Forest Park, five-minute walk to the MRT. You moved from San Francisco. You are paying less rent and eating better food.

8am: Yangmingshan. The bus from Jiantan MRT takes 40 minutes to the Xiaoyoukeng trailhead. The volcanic landscape — steam venting from fumaroles, sulfur yellow on the rock, the view of the Taipei Basin below through morning haze — is otherworldly within a city of 2.5 million. You're back downtown by noon.

Afternoon: the National Palace Museum for two hours. The Jadeite Cabbage is still there, in its case, still the most perfectly carved thing you have ever seen.

Evening: Raohe Night Market. You eat: oyster vermicelli from the stall you've been going to for six months ($3), peppercorn bun from the wood-oven bakery outside Ciyou Temple ($1.20), mango shaved ice ($3). You take the MRT home. Total evening: $12. Taipei on Sunday night is the best city in the world, in your current opinion.

Before You Go

The practical stuff

Taiwan's Gold Card visa is one of Asia's most thoughtfully designed long-stay programs. The language barrier and geopolitical context are the two factors that require honest evaluation.

🛂
Taiwan Gold Card visa — what it covers
Taiwan's Employment Gold Card is a combined work permit, residence permit, and re-entry permit valid for 1–3 years. It was originally designed for tech professionals but has expanded to cover multiple fields. The income-based pathway: applicants who have earned a salary of NTD 160,000/month (~$5,000 USD/month) in the past three years in a qualifying field (finance, technology, law, arts, sports, and others) can qualify. The Gold Card grants the right to live and work freely in Taiwan — no job offer required — and to bring a spouse and dependents. After 5 years of continuous legal residency, permanent residence is available. There is no dedicated passive-income retirement visa like Portugal's D7; the Gold Card's income threshold (NTD 160,000/month) is based on earned income in the past, not ongoing passive income. Retirees who don't meet the Gold Card criteria can apply for a standard long-term visitor visa and renew it, or explore the APRC (Alien Permanent Resident Certificate) after 5 years. Consult a Taiwan immigration specialist for current options — the landscape has evolved and continues to change.
🏥
NHI — Taiwan's universal health coverage
Taiwan's National Health Insurance (NHI) system is consistently rated among the world's best single-payer healthcare systems — comprehensive coverage, low copays, short wait times for outpatient appointments, and full specialist access. Legal residents (including Gold Card holders) become eligible to enroll after 6 consecutive months of residency. Monthly NHI premium for a household with no Taiwan-source income: approximately NTD 1,000–1,600 per person (~$32–50 USD). With NHI: a GP visit copay is NTD 100–150 (~$3–5 USD); a specialist visit NTD 200–500 (~$6–16 USD); hospitalization is covered at a low daily copay. The system covers prescriptions, most surgical procedures, and traditional Chinese medicine at participating clinics. Private supplemental insurance is available for single rooms, extra services, or cross-border coverage. US Medicare does not cover Taiwan — NHI or private insurance is the full picture until you enroll. NHI is one of the most significant financial advantages of Taiwan residency for retirees: genuinely comprehensive health coverage for approximately $35/month per person once enrolled.
🗣️
Mandarin — the language reality
Mandarin Chinese is Taiwan's official language and the language of most daily life outside international business districts. The Taiwanese Mandarin writing system uses Traditional characters (not the Simplified characters used in mainland China). English proficiency among younger Taiwanese in Taipei is improving rapidly — university graduates and professionals under 40 are often conversational in English. In practice, daily life for English-speaking expats in Taipei is manageable but requires genuine language effort: restaurant menus outside tourist areas are typically in Chinese only, grocery store packaging is in Chinese, administrative processes (visa renewals, banking, utility setup) require either Mandarin or a bilingual helper. Learning even 200–300 characters of Traditional Chinese and basic conversational Mandarin substantially improves daily life quality and is strongly recommended before settling. The expat community in Taipei is active and well-networked (Taipei Expats Facebook group, ICRT English radio, Community Services Center) with decades of experience helping new arrivals navigate the practical challenges.

Places with a similar vibe

East Asian urban excellence, world-class food, excellent public transit, and strong healthcare infrastructure. These share Taipei's spirit.

🇲🇾
Penang, Malaysia
Southeast Asia
Also exceptional food culture (George Town is UNESCO World Heritage for its Chinese-Malay-Indian-British culinary heritage), tropical climate rather than subtropical, and Malaysia's MM2H program offers one of Asia's most accessible long-term residency pathways. Lower costs than Taipei; less urban intensity.
🇹🇭
Chiang Mai, Thailand
Northern Thailand
Lower costs than Taipei, Thailand's Non-Immigrant O-A retirement visa is established and accessible at 50+. Different cultural environment (Buddhist Thailand vs. Taiwanese blend), less urban density, no NHI equivalent but private healthcare is excellent and inexpensive. Mountains accessible rather than volcanic park.
🇻🇳
Da Nang, Vietnam
Central Vietnam
Lower costs than Taipei, ocean and mountain access, excellent Vietnamese food culture, and the Marble Mountains and ancient Hoi An within day-trip distance. Vietnam has no long-term retirement visa (border runs required), but the cost-to-lifestyle ratio is exceptional. Less urban infrastructure than Taipei.
