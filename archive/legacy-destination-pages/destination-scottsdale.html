Why People Choose Scottsdale

The vibe in four cards

Scottsdale has the best healthcare of any city on our list — and winters that make everyone who grew up shoveling snow feel quietly vindicated.

🏥
Mayo Clinic — right here
Mayo Clinic's Scottsdale campus is one of three Mayo locations in the US — with the same comprehensive diagnostic and specialty care as the flagship in Rochester. HonorHealth and Banner Health round out a remarkably strong local healthcare system. For retirees who rank medical access near the top of the list, Scottsdale is hard to beat domestically.
☀️
Desert winters that feel like a gift
October through April, Scottsdale is one of the most pleasant places in the country — 65–75°F, sunny, dry, almost no rain. The Sonoran Desert in spring is covered in wildflowers. Camelback Mountain, the McDowell Sonoran Preserve, and Pinnacle Peak are hiking destinations that take your breath away literally and figuratively. This is what people move here for.
🎨
A real arts and dining scene
Old Town Scottsdale has art galleries, restaurants, and a walkable nightlife district that belies its suburb-of-Phoenix location. The Scottsdale Arts District concentrates serious galleries and a thriving First Fridays scene. The dining is genuinely excellent — James Beard-nominated chefs, strong Southwest cuisine, farm-to-table options that reflect the regional food identity.
⛳
Golf, spa, and the resort lifestyle
Scottsdale has more golf courses per capita than almost anywhere in the US — 200+, ranging from world-class resort courses to affordable daily-fee options. The spa resort culture here is also real — world-famous properties like the Boulders, Four Seasons, and Sanctuary attract visitors; residents have access to their facilities at member rates. This is a lifestyle destination that takes leisure seriously.

Life on the Ground

A Thursday in January

You live in a 2BR condo in North Scottsdale — $2,200/mo, mountain views, ten minutes from the Preserve trailheads. The complex has a pool that's warm enough to use nine months out of the year. You moved from Minneapolis eighteen months ago. The first winter here still feels unreal.

Thursday mornings you hike the Tom's Thumb trail in the McDowell Sonoran Preserve — 4.5 miles, 1,000 feet of elevation, views of the entire Valley of the Sun from the top. You're back at your car by 9:30am. The temperature is 68°F. Back home there's a foot of snow.

You grab coffee and a breakfast burrito at a spot in Old Town, then walk the galleries on Marshall Way — you've become a regular at two of them, know the owners now. Lunch with a friend who retired here from Chicago four years ago. He's the one who told you to come. He's never stopped thanking himself for moving.

One honest thing about Scottsdale: summer is punishing. July and August regularly hit 110–115°F. You can't be outside between 9am and sunset. Many retirees leave for 6–8 weeks in summer. If you can't do that, understand what those months feel like. The trade — seven months of perfection — is still worth it for most people who do it.

Before You Go

The practical stuff

Arizona is genuinely favorable for retirees. A few specifics worth knowing.

💰
Arizona's retirement tax picture
Arizona exempts Social Security income from state income tax. The state income tax rate has been cut significantly — dropping to a flat 2.5% for most income in 2023, one of the lowest in the country. Arizona also has a relatively modest property tax rate. No estate or inheritance tax. Overall, a favorable state tax environment for retirees — though not as favorable as Florida or Texas, which have no income tax at all.
🌡️
The summer question
June through mid-September, Phoenix-area temperatures routinely hit 110–115°F. Heat-related illness is a real risk for anyone who underestimates it. Most established Scottsdale retirees have a plan: either leave for 6–8 weeks (Flagstaff is 2hrs away at 7,000 feet, or return north), or stay and adapt (morning activity only, pools, air conditioning). This isn't a dealbreaker — but it needs to be planned for, not discovered.
🏘️
Neighborhoods: a sprawling city
Scottsdale spans 30+ miles north to south. Old Town (central) is walkable and social. North Scottsdale (north of Frank Lloyd Wright Blvd) is newer, more resort-like, higher-end. South Scottsdale and Arcadia (near Camelback) offer more established neighborhoods with older homes. Fountain Hills (east) is quieter, with lake views and lower costs. All require a car. Match the neighborhood to how you want to spend your days.
