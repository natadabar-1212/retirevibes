Why Greenville might be your place

Four reasons this came up at the top.

Greenville has been on more "best places to retire" lists in the past five years than almost any other mid-size American city. It's not hype — there's a real downtown, a real outdoor scene, real affordability, and real four seasons. The question is whether the Southeast is where you want to land.

🌿

A walkable downtown that actually works

Falls Park on the Reedy is the centerpiece — a 26-acre urban park built around a waterfall in the middle of the city, with a pedestrian suspension bridge and walking paths along the river. Main Street has been consistently ranked one of the best walkable main streets in the Southeast: independent restaurants, live music venues, galleries, the Peace Center for the Performing Arts, and a farmers market. This is not a strip mall retirement — it's a city that chose to invest in its public spaces and got something genuinely livable.

🚵

Blue Ridge Mountains, twenty minutes away

Greenville sits at the foot of the Blue Ridge Mountains. The Swamp Rabbit Trail is a 22-mile rail-trail connecting downtown Greenville to Travelers Rest — flat, paved, heavily used by cyclists and walkers. Caesar's Head State Park and Table Rock are 45 minutes away. The Blue Ridge Parkway is an easy day trip. This is four-season outdoors: spring wildflowers, summer ridgeline hiking, fall foliage that rivals New England, and winter that's cold but not punishing. For people who want the mountains but not the isolation, Greenville threads that needle.

💲

Meaningfully cheaper than most comparable cities

Greenville's cost of living runs about 8–10% below the US national average, and 25–40% below comparable-quality cities like Asheville, Austin, Denver, or Portland. Median home prices are around $360K — far below the national median for similar quality of life. Property taxes on a primary residence are among the lowest in the country: roughly $2,400/year on a $400K home under South Carolina's 4% primary residence assessment. Social Security income is not taxed by South Carolina. The math on retiring here is meaningfully better than most of the places people are moving from.

🏥

Strong healthcare infrastructure

Prisma Health — the Upstate South Carolina health system — operates multiple hospitals and medical campuses in the Greenville metro. Bon Secours St. Francis is the other major system. Both are full-service, with cardiac, cancer, orthopedic, and neurology programs. Medicare is accepted everywhere. The University of South Carolina School of Medicine Greenville graduates physicians who stay in the region, keeping the specialist supply robust. For Americans who want quality domestic healthcare as a non-negotiable, Greenville delivers it without the big-city premium.

What a Saturday in October feels like.

The leaves on the trees along Falls Park have been turning for two weeks. October in Greenville is the month people who've moved here tell their friends about — 65 degrees and sunny, the kind of fall that the Southeast doesn't always deliver but Upstate South Carolina reliably does. You walked from your place on Augusta Road in 12 minutes. The suspension bridge over the waterfall has a line for photos at this hour, which you've learned to navigate by coming early.

Farmers market at the TD Saturday Market on Main Street. You buy a pumpkin bread from the bakery you've been getting it from since September, and talk to the couple from the next stall — they're from Michigan, moved here three years ago, same trajectory as you. Lunch at a restaurant where you know the owner by name, which happened faster here than in most cities you've lived in.

Afternoon: a 14-mile bike ride on the Swamp Rabbit Trail to Travelers Rest and back. The mountains are visible the whole way, pale blue in the distance. Your daughter is visiting from Boston next weekend and you're already planning which hike to do at Table Rock. It's two hours away, which feels close now. Everything here feels closer than it used to.

The practical stuff

Three things to know before you go further.

Taxes

South Carolina is retiree-friendly

South Carolina does not tax Social Security income. The state also exempts a portion of retirement income (up to $15,000 per person for those 65+, indexed annually). South Carolina's income tax rate is being phased down from 6.5% to 3% by 2027. Property taxes on a primary residence are assessed at 4% of fair market value — among the lowest rates in the US. On a $360K home, annual property tax runs roughly $2,400, or about $200/month. No estate tax in South Carolina.

Healthcare

Medicare accepted everywhere — plan well

Greenville's two major health systems — Prisma Health and Bon Secours St. Francis — both accept Medicare. Medicare Advantage plans are widely available in the Greenville market from major national insurers. The Greenville metro has a strong primary care base and good specialist access in most areas. If you have specific ongoing specialist needs, confirm provider availability before committing. Dental and vision remain the main gaps — budget for standalone coverage or a dedicated savings buffer.

Neighborhoods

Where you land matters

Downtown and North Main are the most walkable areas — close to Falls Park, Main Street, and the farmers market. Augusta Road is charming, slightly more suburban. Travelers Rest (15 min north) gives you a small-town feel with mountain proximity. Simpsonville and Mauldin to the south are more suburban and affordable. The difference between living walkably near downtown and living 10 minutes out is significant — worth spending a week in different areas on a scouting trip before deciding which neighborhood is actually yours.
