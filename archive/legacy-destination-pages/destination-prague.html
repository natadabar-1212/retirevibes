🇨🇿 Prague, Czech Republic

Gothic spires, a beer culture older than nations, and one of Europe's great old towns

Prague survived the 20th century's wars largely intact — which means the medieval Old Town, the Baroque Malá Strana, the Gothic St. Vitus Cathedral, and the Charles Bridge are all still there, exactly as they were built. The city has a density of architectural history that rivals anywhere in Europe, at Central European prices. Czech beer, brewed according to tradition since the 13th century, is still the finest in the world in the opinion of anyone who drinks it here. The Vltava divides the city into neighborhoods with distinct characters. The winters are cold and the summers are warm and the in-between is extraordinary.

Why People Choose Prague

The vibe in four cards

Prague works best for people who want serious European cultural depth — architecture, music, literature, history — combined with costs that are well below Western European capitals. It is a city of genuine intellectual and aesthetic substance.

🏰
The old town — intact medieval Europe
Prague's historic core — the Old Town (Staré Město), Lesser Town (Malá Strana), and Hradčany (castle district) — is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the best-preserved medieval urban fabrics in the world. The Astronomical Clock (Orloj, 1410) in Old Town Square is still operational. Charles Bridge (Karlův most, 1357), crossing the Vltava with its 30 Baroque statues, remains pedestrian-only. St. Vitus Cathedral inside Prague Castle contains the tomb of St. Wenceslas (patron saint of Bohemia) and the Bohemian crown jewels. The Jewish Quarter (Josefov) has six functioning synagogues and one of Europe's oldest Jewish cemeteries (burials from 1439). Prague escaped the worst of World War II bombing that destroyed much of Warsaw, Dresden, and other Central European cities, leaving a layered urban fabric of Gothic, Renaissance, Baroque, Art Nouveau, and Cubist architecture that has no real parallel in size or quality outside a handful of Italian cities.
🍺
Czech beer — at the source of Pilsner
The Czech Republic invented the Pilsner style in 1842 — Pilsner Urquell was first brewed in Plzeň (Pilsen), 90km west of Prague. Czech per-capita beer consumption is the highest in the world; the pub (hospoda) is not merely a bar but a social institution, a living room, a neighborhood anchor. Pivnice U Fleků (founded 1499, still brewing its dark lager on-premises) is the oldest continuously operating brewery-restaurant in Prague. Lokál, Zly Časy, and Pivovarský dům are among the modern classics. A half-liter (půllitr) of Pilsner Urquell or Kozel at a genuine Czech pub: €1.20–1.80. Not a special deal — the standard price. Czech beer culture at this quality and this price has no equivalent in Western Europe, and this single fact about Prague daily life matters considerably to a certain type of person.
🎭
Classical music, opera, and cultural life
Prague has one of the densest classical music and opera scenes in Europe relative to its size. The Prague State Opera (Státní opera) — a 1888 neo-Rococo theater — runs full opera and ballet seasons. The National Theatre (Národní divadlo) opened in 1881 and stages Czech opera, ballet, and drama. Rudolfinum concert hall hosts the Czech Philharmonic. Smetana Hall in the Municipal House (Obecní dům — Art Nouveau masterpiece, 1912) is one of Europe's finest concert venues. Prague Spring International Music Festival (May–June) is a major annual event. The entire system is heavily subsidized — good opera tickets cost €15–50, and last-minute standing room or upper tier seats are available for €5–10 at the state theaters. For classical music and opera enthusiasts, Prague delivers world-class performances at prices that represent a fraction of Vienna, London, or New York costs. This is a significant factor in the quality of cultural life available here.
🌿
Riverside neighborhoods and green city life
Prague is a genuinely green city — the Vltava riverbanks, Stromovka park (a former royal hunting ground), Letná park (with views over the Old Town rooftops), Divoká Šárka (a natural ravine with swimming lake in the northwest), and Vinohrady's leafy residential streets all provide urban green space at a quality that rivals Paris or Vienna. Riegrovy Sady in Vinohrady has a beer garden with arguably the best view of the Prague Castle in the city. The Vltava embankment (Náplavka) on summer evenings becomes an outdoor market and social gathering point, with farmers' markets on Saturdays and floating bars moored to the bank. Cycling infrastructure has improved substantially in recent years, and the city is increasingly bikeable for residents. Prague's residential neighborhoods — Vinohrady, Žižkov, Holešovice, Dejvice — are genuinely livable at a quality that the tourist-facing Old Town doesn't represent, and most long-term expats live in these neighborhoods rather than the historic center.

Life on the Ground

A Saturday between the castle and Vinohrady

You rent a 2BR apartment in Vinohrady — €1,100/month, second floor of a 1910 Art Nouveau building, tiled floors, high ceilings, balcony over a linden-tree-lined street. You moved from Seattle. Seattle had better coffee. Prague has everything else.

8am: coffee and a rohlík (a Czech crescent roll, €0.20) from the bakery on the corner. Then the tram across the river to Malá Strana before the tourist wave. Charles Bridge at 8:30am in September with fog on the Vltava and no one but a few fishermen: this is the version of the bridge that belongs to people who live here.

Up to Hradčany on foot. The castle complex opens at 9am. You've been here a dozen times and still stop at the Golden Lane (Zlatá ulička, 16th-century cottages built into the castle fortifications where Franz Kafka briefly rented a room to write in 1916). You stop every time.

Afternoon: Riegrovy Sady beer garden with the castle visible across the city. A půllitr of Kozel, €1.50. Dinner in Žižkov — a neighborhood restaurant, svíčková (braised beef with cream sauce and bread dumplings), Czech wine from Moravia, €28 for two. Back home on the tram by 10pm. Prague is very good on Saturday nights when you don't have to be anywhere specific.

Before You Go

The practical stuff

The Czech Republic is a Schengen member — the 90-day rule applies to US tourists. Long-term residency requires a national (D-type) long-stay visa, which is more bureaucratically involved than Portugal's D7.

🛂
Czech Republic residency for Americans
The Czech Republic is a Schengen member, meaning US citizens can stay 90 days in any 180-day period without a visa — the same rule that applies across the Schengen zone. For stays beyond 90 days, US citizens need a Czech national long-stay visa (D visa), which requires a stated purpose: employment, self-employment (živnostenský list — trade license), study, family reunification, or other approved categories. There is no straightforward passive-income retirement visa comparable to Portugal's D7 or Spain's NLV. The most common pathway for non-EU retirees settling in Prague is obtaining a long-stay visa as a self-employed person (even with minimal business activity) or via family reunification if a family member is an EU citizen. EU citizens have freedom of movement. This visa complexity is Prague's main disadvantage vs. Iberian Peninsula destinations — the bureaucratic pathway is less clean for ordinary American retirees. Consult an immigration attorney with Czech practice before planning a permanent move; requirements and processing times change.
🏥
Healthcare in Prague
Prague has strong healthcare infrastructure — Motol University Hospital (the Czech Republic's largest and primary university hospital), Všeobecná fakultní nemocnice (VFN — General University Hospital in New Town), and a well-developed private sector. Canadian Medical Centre, Medicover, and Ústřední vojenská nemocnice (Central Military Hospital) all serve expats with English-speaking staff. Private GP consultation: €50–100. Specialist: €80–160. Legal residents with Czech health insurance (povinné zdravotní pojištění) access the public system, which is of good quality. Non-residents on tourist stays pay out of pocket. International health insurance for a couple in their 50s: $2,500–5,000/year. Dental care in Prague is significantly less expensive than in the US — Czech dentistry has attracted "dental tourism" from Germany and Austria for years. US Medicare does not cover Prague.
❄️
Winter — cold, dark, and beautiful
Prague's winter is genuinely Central European — average January temperatures of 0–3°C (32–37°F), with periods below freezing, frost, occasional snow, and short days (sunset at 4pm in December). This is not debilitating cold — it's the cold of a European city with functioning heating, good public transit, excellent indoor restaurant and cafe culture, and a Christmas market season (December) that is among the finest in Europe. The Prague Christmas Market on Old Town Square runs through December; the mulled wine (svařák), trdelník (chimney cake), and carolers in the medieval square are genuinely, unselfconsciously festive. The Czech phrase is "Czech it off your bucket list" — which would be funny if it weren't true. People who love Prague winters love the opera, the warm pubs, the amber light on the old stones, and the fog on the Vltava. People who struggle are those who require outdoor life or sunshine as a daily requirement.

Places with a similar vibe

Central European grandeur, affordable costs, classical culture, four seasons. These share the Prague character.

🇭🇺
Budapest, Hungary
Central Europe
Art Nouveau rather than Gothic/Baroque, thermal baths rather than beer halls, slightly less expensive than Prague. Also a Schengen member with the same 90-day tourist rule. Budapest's Guest Investor Program is the current residency pathway; no clean income-based retirement visa either.
🇵🇹
Porto, Portugal
Western Europe
For those drawn to Prague's European character and costs but who want a cleaner residency pathway — Porto's D7 visa is the most accessible long-stay option in Western Europe for American retirees. Milder Atlantic climate, similar cost range, wine culture instead of beer culture.
🇭🇷
Split, Croatia
Adriatic Europe
Also affordable European heritage city at Central European prices, but warmer — Adriatic Mediterranean climate rather than Continental. Croatia D visa offers a more accessible long-stay pathway for Americans than Czech Republic currently provides. Less cultural density than Prague but dramatically more sun.
