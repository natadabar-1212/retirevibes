Why Costa Rica might be your place

Four reasons this came up at the top.

Costa Rica has been the most popular retirement destination in Latin America for Americans for over two decades — and for good reason. The infrastructure is built for this. The question is which town fits your pace.

🌤️

72 degrees. Every day. No hyperbole.

The Central Valley sits at 3,000–4,000 feet of elevation, which means it misses the coastal heat and humidity that surprises first-time visitors. Atenas, the small coffee town 45 minutes from San José, has been called one of the best climates in the world — mild, green, and sunny most of the year. No air conditioning required. No heat bill either. The rainy season (May–November) brings daily afternoon showers that last an hour and then stop. The rest of the day is glorious.

🛂

The lowest retirement visa bar anywhere

Costa Rica's Pensionado visa requires just $1,000 per month in pension, Social Security, or annuity income — the lowest documented retirement visa income requirement in the world. There's no minimum age, no health requirement, and no language test. The visa renews every two years, and you can apply for permanent residency after three years. For Americans already drawing any Social Security or pension, this is one of the easiest legal paths to long-term residency abroad.

🏥

World-class hospitals, not world-class prices

CIMA Hospital in Escazú is affiliated with the Cleveland Clinic and holds Joint Commission International accreditation — the same standard as top US hospitals. Clínica Bíblica and Hospital La Católica in San José are equally well regarded. Procedures that cost tens of thousands in the US run a fraction of that here. Once you're a legal resident, you can enroll in CAJA — the public universal healthcare system — for about 7–11% of reported income per month. Most American retirees supplement with a private plan that costs a few hundred dollars a month for comprehensive coverage.

✈️

Close enough to feel like a choice, not a sacrifice

SJO (Juan Santamaría International Airport) outside San José has direct flights to Miami, Houston, Dallas, Los Angeles, New York, Chicago, and Atlanta — most under five hours. It's a three-hour flight to Miami. That proximity matters enormously for people with aging parents, grandchildren, or ongoing medical relationships in the US. You're not trading family for adventure; you're just moving to a different time zone. And USD is accepted almost everywhere in the Central Valley, so the currency transition is gentler than most international moves.

What a Saturday in Atenas feels like.

You're up before 8, which surprised you at first — you were never a morning person back home. But the light comes in differently here, and it's already 72 degrees with a clear sky. You walk five minutes to the feria, the weekly farmers market in the central park. Avocados, papayas, fresh tortillas, a bag of coffee beans from a finca up the road. Total: $12.

You've been going to the same soda — a small Tico lunch counter — for six months now. The casado is $7: rice, beans, salad, fried plantains, and your choice of chicken, fish, or the vegetable option your neighbor from Minnesota swears by. You've started recognizing the regulars. The woman who owns it speaks no English and you speak functional Spanish. You manage fine.

Afternoon is slow. You work in the garden for an hour. You video call your daughter in Austin — she keeps trying to make the travel math work for a visit in January. The light turns golden around 5pm, the way it does in places that are at the right altitude. Your neighbor texts: fire pit tonight, 7pm. You don't need to bring anything. Tomorrow you're thinking about driving up to Poás volcano. It's 45 minutes and you still haven't gone.

The practical stuff

Three things to know before you go further.

Visa

Pensionado — the world's easiest bar

The Pensionado visa requires $1,000/month in documented pension, Social Security, or annuity income — the lowest retirement visa income threshold of any country that actively courts American retirees. No age minimum. The visa is issued for two years, renewable indefinitely, and you can apply for permanent residency after three years. The Rentista visa ($2,500/month or $60K lump sum invested) covers people without pension income.

Healthcare

CAJA public + JCI-accredited private

Legal residents can enroll in CAJA, Costa Rica's public universal healthcare system, for about 7–11% of reported monthly income. This gives access to a nationwide network of hospitals and clinics. For faster access and English-speaking providers, most American expats supplement with private care at CIMA Hospital (Cleveland Clinic affiliate), Clínica Bíblica, or Hospital La Católica — all JCI-accredited. A comprehensive private plan for two adults typically runs $200–$400/month depending on age and coverage level.

Taxes

Foreign income not taxed — but no treaty

Costa Rica does not tax foreign-sourced income — your US Social Security, pension, and investment income won't be taxed by Costa Rica. However, there's no US–Costa Rica tax treaty, which means your US tax obligations don't change. You'll still file a US return and report foreign accounts over the FBAR threshold. Get a cross-border CPA who knows both sides before you finalize anything. The combination of low local taxation and no treaty is navigable — it just needs the right professional.
