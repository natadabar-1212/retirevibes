🇰🇳 St. Kitts & Nevis · Eastern Caribbean

St. Kitts,
timeless.

A volcanic island of green mountains, colonial history, and unhurried Caribbean life — home to the oldest citizenship by investment program in the world, and a growing community of people who chose it deliberately.

🏔️ Volcanic Peaks 🏰 Colonial History 🗣️ English Speaking 🏡 Citizenship by Investment
The Vibe

Small island.
Big life.

St. Kitts is 68 square miles — small enough to know intimately, large enough to feel like it has real layers. The volcanic central spine rises to over 3,000 feet, draped in rainforest. The coast drops quickly to black sand beaches on the Atlantic side and calm Caribbean beaches to the west. The scenery is dramatically different depending on which direction you face.

Brimstone Hill Fortress — a UNESCO World Heritage Site perched on a volcanic plug overlooking the sea — is one of the best-preserved colonial fortifications in the Americas. The island's sugar cane history is visible everywhere: old mill chimneys rising from the countryside, a scenic narrow-gauge railway that once served the plantations now running tourist excursions around the island.

The expat community is international and intentional — people who weighed their options and chose St. Kitts for specific reasons. Frigate Bay has developed into a social hub with beach bars, restaurants, and a sailing crowd that makes weekends feel festive without being overwhelming. The whole island is navigable in 20 minutes.

What You'll Love

Life in St. Kitts

🏡

The World's Oldest CBI Program

St. Kitts launched the first citizenship by investment program in the Caribbean in 1984. It's the most established, most scrutinized, and most trusted program of its kind. Kittitian citizenship includes visa-free access to 150+ countries including the UK and Schengen zone.

🏔️

Dramatic Mountain Scenery

Mt. Liamuiga (3,792 ft) is a dormant volcano you can hike to the crater rim. The rainforest interior is one of the most intact in the Eastern Caribbean. The contrast between volcanic highlands and Caribbean beaches is genuinely stunning.

🏰

Deep History

Brimstone Hill Fortress is a UNESCO World Heritage Site — one of the best-preserved colonial military monuments in the Americas. The island's role in Caribbean history gives it a texture and depth that newer resort destinations lack.

🎿

The Strip & Frigate Bay

Frigate Bay's "Strip" — a row of beach bars on the calm Caribbean side — is the social heart of expat life on the island. Wednesday night at Vibes, Friday at Carambola Beach Club. A scene that feels local rather than manufactured for tourists.

⛵

Nevis Just Across the Channel

The sister island of Nevis is a 45-minute ferry ride away — quieter, more rural, home to the Four Seasons resort and some of the most private real estate in the Caribbean. Having two islands accessible adds dimension to life here.

🗣️

English Language & Common Law

English is the official language. The legal system is based on British common law — familiar and navigable for Americans. Property transactions, residency applications, and banking are all conducted in English with a legal framework that makes sense.

Where to Land

Areas & Communities

St. Kitts is compact — the whole island is navigable quickly. But where you settle shapes your daily rhythm.

Frigate Bay

Most Social

The primary expat hub — resort hotels, condos, restaurants, and the Strip beach bars all concentrated in one area. Active, social, and the default choice for people who want built-in community from day one. The dual-beach peninsula (Atlantic on one side, Caribbean on the other) is a unique feature.

Southeast Peninsula

Most Scenic

The long, undeveloped peninsula at the island's southern tip — home to some of the most dramatic beaches, protected salt pond lagoons, and the island's newest luxury development. Quieter and more private than Frigate Bay. Incredible views toward Nevis.

Basseterre

Most Authentic

The capital — a small, genuinely working Caribbean town with a central Circus (roundabout) modeled on Piccadilly Circus, a waterfront, and all government and banking services. Living here puts you in the middle of local Kittitian life rather than the expat bubble.

North Shore Villages

Most Quiet

The villages along the northern Caribbean coast — Dieppe Bay, Sandy Point, Half Way Tree — are slower, more local, and significantly less expensive. For people who want to live among Kittitians rather than expats, this is where that life exists.

Know Before You Go

What to plan for

Citizenship by Investment

St. Kitts's CBI program offers citizenship through a qualifying real estate investment (from $400,000 in designated developments) or a Sustainable Island State Contribution (from $250,000). Kittitian citizenship carries visa-free or visa-on-arrival access to 150+ countries. Processing takes 3–6 months. Work with a licensed CBI agent.

Residency Without CBI

You don't need to pursue citizenship to retire here. A temporary residency permit is available for retirees who can demonstrate sufficient income, and can be renewed annually. Many expats live here long-term on residency without pursuing citizenship.

Healthcare

Joseph N. France General Hospital in Basseterre covers essential care. Several private clinics operate on the island. For complex procedures — cardiac, oncology, surgery — Antigua and Barbados are the go-to regional hubs. A 45-minute flight covers most medical travel needs. International health insurance with evacuation coverage is standard.

Eastern Caribbean Dollar

St. Kitts uses the EC Dollar (XCD), pegged at 2.70 to the USD since 1976 — one of the most stable currency arrangements in the Western Hemisphere. USD is widely accepted at tourist-facing businesses. Local banking is available; maintaining a US account in parallel is common practice.

Hurricane Season

The Eastern Caribbean sees meaningful hurricane activity June–November. St. Kitts has been impacted several times historically. Modern construction standards have improved significantly. Long-term residents maintain hurricane protocols, wind insurance, and storm supply stores. The risk is real and manageable with preparation.

Getting Around

St. Kitts drives on the left — British influence, though cars are left-hand drive American-style. A car is essentially required; the island is small but spread out. Taxis are available but expensive for daily use. The scenic railway runs periodic excursions around the island's perimeter — a remarkable way to see the landscape.
