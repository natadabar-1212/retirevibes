Why People Choose Tucson

The vibe in four cards

Tucson isn't Scottsdale's cheaper cousin — it's a genuinely different city with its own identity, its own landscape, and its own reasons for being here. Retirees who find it tend to be fiercely loyal to it.

🌵
Saguaro National Park — on both sides
Saguaro National Park is split into two units, one on each side of the city — East (Rincon Mountain) and West (Tucson Mountain). You can drive to the park entrance in 20 minutes from most neighborhoods. The giant saguaro cacti, some over 150 years old and 50 feet tall, frame every desert hike and every sunset. It's not scenery you get used to.
🎓
University energy without the crowds
The University of Arizona brings 50,000 students, Division I athletics, a performing arts center, an observatory, and a genuine intellectual energy to what would otherwise be a mid-size desert city. The UA Museum of Art, Arizona State Museum, and regular world-class performances at Centennial Hall give Tucson cultural programming well beyond its size. Retirees who want to keep learning find the University a genuine asset.
🌄
Five mountain ranges surround the city
Tucson sits in a basin ringed by five distinct mountain ranges — the Rincons, Catalinas, Tucson Mountains, Santa Ritas, and Tortolitas. Mt. Lemmon in the Catalinas rises to 9,157 feet — a ski area 27 miles from downtown that gets real snow in winter while the city stays warm. Sabino Canyon, 7 Cataracts, and Bear Canyon offer hiking that requires no travel at all.
🌮
Authentic Sonoran food culture
Tucson was the first US city designated a UNESCO City of Gastronomy — for its indigenous and Sonoran food traditions, not for its trendy restaurants. Carne seca. Sonoran hot dogs. Chiltepín chiles. Mesquite-grilled meats. The food here has a 4,000-year culinary history, and the restaurants (from El Guero Canelo to Café Poca Cosa) honor it. James Beard has noticed. So should you.

Life on the Ground

A Friday morning in the desert

You live in a 2BR home in the Foothills — $420K, mountain views, no HOA. The Santa Catalinas are literally the backdrop to your backyard. You moved from Boston two years ago. You have not thought about a snow blower since.

Friday mornings you drive 10 minutes to Sabino Canyon. You hike the Phoneline Trail — 6 miles along the canyon rim, views into the canyon below, the city spread out to the south. You're back in the car by 8:30am, before the heat arrives. In January, you hike at 10am. In July, you're done by 7. The desert teaches you to move with the sun.

Afternoons: a lecture series at the UA this week — a geologist talking about the Sonoran Desert's formation. $12. Dinner at a spot on Fourth Avenue: green corn tamales, carne asada, a local pale ale. $38 for two. Your neighbor, a retired professor from Ohio who's been here for twelve years, waves from across the patio. He says Tucson is the best-kept secret in American retirement. He says it less quietly every year.

Tomorrow you're driving to Kartchner Caverns, a world-class living cave 45 minutes away. Sunday, Biosphere 2 is doing a tour. There is always something here — the city keeps surprising you with what it has to offer.

Before You Go

The practical stuff

Same state as Scottsdale — same tax rules, similar healthcare quality, very different city character. Here's what to know.

💰
Arizona's retirement tax picture
Arizona exempts Social Security from state income tax. The flat state income tax is 2.5% — one of the lowest in the US. No estate or inheritance tax. Property taxes in Tucson (Pima County) are relatively low, and the homestead exemption reduces the assessed value of primary residences. Overall, a favorable tax environment — the same as Scottsdale, at a lower cost of living.
🏥
Healthcare: university-affiliated anchor
Banner-University Medical Center Tucson is affiliated with the University of Arizona Health Sciences — a teaching hospital with research depth and specialty care that most cities of Tucson's size don't have. Tucson Medical Center (TMC) is the other major regional hospital. For complex cases, the UA connection means Tucson has access to trials and specialists. Medicare works at both. Tucson punches above its weight medically.
🌡️
Summer — real but manageable
Tucson gets hot in summer — July highs around 100–104°F, which is several degrees cooler than Scottsdale's 110–115°F. The monsoon season (July-September) brings afternoon thunderstorms and cooler post-storm temps — Tucsonans actually look forward to the monsoons. The higher elevation (2,389 ft vs. Scottsdale's 1,250 ft) makes a real difference. Still: morning outdoor activity only in summer, and budget for high electric bills for AC.
