Why Hua Hin

Royal by reputation,
easy by nature

Hua Hin has been Thailand's most beloved beach retreat since the 1920s, when the royal family made it their seaside escape. That legacy shows — this is a town that takes leisure seriously. Long stretches of clean beach, more than 20 golf courses within an hour's drive, a vibrant night market, and an expat community that's settled and sociable.

Unlike the party beaches of the south, Hua Hin moves at a pace that suits retirement. It's a place to wake up without an agenda: coffee, a beach walk, a few hours of golf, and a fresh seafood dinner as the sun dips into the Gulf of Thailand. And with Bangkok just three hours away by train or road, the city is always there when you want it.

Hua Hin costs roughly a third of what a comfortable coastal lifestyle runs in Florida — without giving up much in the way of amenities, weather, or quality of life.

The Vibe

What life actually feels like

🏖️

Beach Without the Chaos

Hua Hin's 5-kilometer main beach is wide, clean, and nowhere near as crowded as Phuket or Koh Samui. You can stake out your spot, take a morning swim, and be back for breakfast by 8am. Farther south, Khao Takiab and Pranburi offer even quieter sands for days when you want to disappear entirely.

⛳

Golf Capital of Thailand

With over 20 championship courses within an hour's drive — including Black Mountain, Banyan, and Springfield Royal — Hua Hin is legitimately one of Asia's great golf destinations. Greens fees run $30–80 USD for rounds that would cost 3–4x that in Florida or Arizona. Caddies are standard and affordable.

🦐

Food That Never Disappoints

The Hua Hin Night Market is one of Thailand's best — 300 stalls of fresh seafood, pad thai, mango sticky rice, and more, all for a few dollars. For something more sit-down, the town has a thriving café scene, excellent Italian, and fresh catch restaurants right on the waterfront. You will not run out of places to eat.

🌿

An Expat Community That's Settled In

Hua Hin has attracted expats from Europe, Australia, and North America for decades. There are established social clubs, a Rotary chapter, weekly hash runs, tennis groups, and book clubs. The community is warm without being cliquey — it's easy to meet people, and easier to find your niche within the first few months.

A Day in the Life

Your Tuesday in Hua Hin

6:30 am

Beach Walk & Market Breakfast

An hour before the heat builds, you're on the main beach — barefoot, watching kite surfers and local fishermen haul in the morning catch. Breakfast is at a market stall near the old train station: khao tom (rice soup), fresh-squeezed orange juice, and excellent Thai coffee for less than $3.

10:00 am

18 Holes at Black Mountain

Tee time at one of Southeast Asia's most acclaimed courses. With a caddie carrying your bag and an ice-cold towel at every hole, this is golf as it's meant to be played. Done by 2pm, you head to the clubhouse for a cold Singha and a bowl of noodles, rehashing the round with your playing partners.

4:00 pm

Pool, Sundowner & Night Market

Back home by the pool as the afternoon heat softens. By 6pm, the temperature is perfect. You walk to the night market, work your way through the stalls — grilled prawns, som tum, fresh pineapple — and are back on your terrace with a gin and tonic by 9pm, watching the moon over the Gulf.

Where to Live

Neighborhoods worth knowing

Most Popular

Central Hua Hin

The heart of town — walkable to the beach, night market, and Cicada Market. A mix of condos and shophouses, with everything you need within a 10-minute radius. Busier, but vibrant and social.

Quiet & Residential

Khao Takiab

South of center, anchored by a Buddhist temple on a rocky headland. Quieter beach, local restaurants, and a more authentic Thai neighborhood feel. Popular with long-term retirees who've moved past the center.

Up & Coming

Pranburi

30 minutes south, Pranburi has some of Thailand's best unspoiled beaches and mangrove forests. Lower prices, more space, and a growing community of expats who prefer nature over nightlife.

Healthcare

Private Care, World-Class Quality

Hua Hin has two well-regarded private hospitals: Bangkok Hospital Hua Hin and San Paulo Hospital. Both have English-speaking doctors, modern facilities, and costs a fraction of US equivalents — a specialist consultation runs $25–50 USD, and most procedures are significantly less expensive than in the West.

For complex procedures or surgery, Bangkok (3 hours) is home to some of Asia's top-ranked hospitals including Bumrungrad International, consistently rated among the best in the world. Most expats carry international health insurance and use it as needed.

Bangkok Hospital HHQ San Paulo Hospital Bumrungrad (Bangkok)
Visas & Residency

Non-Immigrant OA (Retirement Visa)

Thailand's Non-Immigrant OA visa is designed for retirees 50 and older. It requires proof of a Thai bank balance (approx. 800,000 THB / ~$23,000 USD), or a monthly pension/income of at least 65,000 THB (~$1,850/month). The visa is valid for one year and renewable annually from within Thailand.

Thailand's newer Long-Term Resident (LTR) visa offers a 10-year option for those meeting higher income thresholds ($40,000+/year passive income). It includes work-from-Thailand privileges and a simplified renewal process — popular with semi-retired professionals.
