Why People Choose Athens

The vibe in four cards

Athens is the underdog of European retirement destinations. It's not as hyped as Lisbon or Barcelona — which means fewer crowds, lower costs, and a city that still belongs to the people who live there.

🏛️
History you can walk through
The Acropolis is visible from much of the city — it's the backdrop to everyday life, not a tourist attraction you visit once. Plaka, the Agora, Kerameikos, the National Archaeological Museum — Athens has more concentrated ancient history than anywhere else on Earth, and most of it is walkable.
⛵
200 islands, ferry ride away
Piraeus port is 30 minutes from the city center. From there, ferries run to Hydra (1.5hrs), Aegina (40 min), Mykonos, Santorini, Crete, Rhodes, and dozens of smaller islands. Living in Athens means the Greek islands aren't a vacation — they're a long weekend.
🍷
A food scene that surprised the world
Athens has quietly become one of Europe's most interesting dining cities — with more Michelin stars than most people expect, a thriving street food scene (souvlaki, spanakopita, loukoumades), and neighborhood tavernas that cost $20 for a table full of mezes and a carafe of house wine.
✈️
More US routes than you'd think
Athens International (ATH) has direct routes from New York (JFK, EWR), Boston, Philadelphia, Chicago (seasonally), Atlanta, and Los Angeles — with carriers including Delta, United, American, and Olympic. Getting back to the US is a direct flight, not a European hub connection.

Life on the Ground

A Sunday under the Acropolis

You live in Koukaki — the neighborhood directly south of the Acropolis, where apartments are nicer than in Plaka and half the price. Your 2BR is €950/mo. From your terrace, on a clear morning, you can see the Parthenon lit up against the early sky.

Sunday mornings, you walk to the Monastiraki flea market. Not to buy anything in particular — just to move through it. Vintage dealers, honey vendors, a man selling komboloi worry beads. You pick up a bougatsa (cream-filled pastry) from a bakery on the way: €2. You've stopped converting prices in your head.

Afternoon, you take the metro to Piraeus and catch the hydrofoil to Hydra for the day. No cars on the island — just donkeys, boats, and whitewashed stairs. You eat grilled octopus on a dock and take the boat back at sunset. You're home by 9pm. Total cost of the day trip: about €80 for two.

Next weekend you're thinking about Aegina. The one after that, maybe nothing. That's the thing about Athens — the city itself is enough. The islands are just a bonus.

Before You Go

The practical stuff

Greece's path for non-EU retirees is newer and less established than Spain's or Portugal's — but it works. Here's what to know.

📋
The Financially Independent Person (FIP) permit
Greece doesn't have a dedicated "retirement visa" — instead, non-EU residents use the Financially Independent Person residence permit. Requirements: approximately €2,000/mo in passive income from abroad (pension, SS, investments), plus private health insurance. Applied for at a Greek consulate in the US, then processed in Greece. Valid for 2 years, renewable. Work in Greece is not permitted. Consult an immigration attorney — requirements and processing times vary.
🏥
Healthcare: strong private sector
Greece's public healthcare (ESY) has had well-documented capacity strains — most expats opt for private care, which is good quality and significantly cheaper than US prices. Hygeia Hospital and Metropolitan Hospital are the top private facilities in Athens, with English-speaking staff. A specialist visit: €60–100. US Medicare does not cover you in Greece. Private health insurance runs €150–350/mo depending on age.
💰
Taxes: attractive flat-rate option
Greece offers a flat €100,000/year tax regime for high-net-worth individuals (not typical for most retirees). More relevant: Greece has a 7% flat income tax rate for foreign pensioners who transfer their tax residency to Greece — introduced to attract retirees. This applies to pension income and is valid for 15 years. The US-Greece tax treaty helps avoid double taxation on many income types. A Greek tax advisor is strongly recommended.
