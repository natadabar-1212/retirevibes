Why the Algarve might be your place

Four reasons this came up at the top.

Portugal's southern coast has been attracting Northern European retirees for 40 years. The infrastructure is built for exactly this. The question isn't whether it works — it's which town works for you.

🏖️

The most dramatic Atlantic beaches in Europe

The Algarve's coastline is something else entirely — towering golden sandstone cliffs, sea caves, grottoes, and hidden cove beaches only reachable by boat. Praia da Marinha, Ponta da Piedade, Praia do Camilo. This isn't the flat sandy Mediterranean; it's the Atlantic doing something genuinely spectacular. And there are 300 sunny days a year to appreciate it.

🇬🇧

Built-in English-speaking community

The British and Northern European expat community in the Algarve is one of the largest and most established in Europe — 40+ years of infrastructure means English-speaking doctors, lawyers, accountants, estate agents, social clubs, and golf courses. For Americans, this removes a lot of the friction of an international move. You can build a fully functional life here without learning much Portuguese in year one.

☀️

Portugal's sunniest, warmest corner

The Algarve averages 300 days of sunshine and is consistently the warmest part of Portugal — milder winters than Porto or Lisbon, with summer temperatures that stay comfortable rather than scorching. The sea temperature (Atlantic, not Mediterranean) means the beach swimming season runs April through October, and the countryside is incredibly lush in spring.

🏘️

Towns with real choices

The Algarve isn't one place — it's a 100-mile string of towns with very different personalities. Lagos: charming historic center, lively café scene, younger international crowd. Tavira: quieter, more Portuguese, Roman bridge and red-tiled rooftops. Faro: the actual capital, university city, most local feel. Silves: inland, medieval castle, half the price. There's a right answer for most people in here somewhere.

What a Tuesday in Lagos feels like.

The morning is slow and very quiet. You walk into town along the old city wall — the 16th-century fortifications are just the path to the bakery. Pastéis de nata, a bica, a newspaper in Portuguese you can half-read now, six months in. The square is warm by 9am.

Late morning at Meia Praia — the long flat beach east of town, less dramatic than the coves but easier to reach on foot. You swim. In October. This is the point. You eat lunch at a table with a view of the sea: grilled sea bream, a glass of white Alentejano, €16. On the way home you pick up groceries at the local market.

Afternoon in the garden, reading. Your neighbor two houses over — a retired architect from London — waves from his terrace. You've both been here four years now and have an easy rhythm. Dinner at home. The light fades slowly over the hills to the east. Tomorrow you're planning to drive to Sagres, the southwestern tip of Europe, because you still haven't been, and it's only 25 minutes away.

The practical stuff

Three things to know before you go further.

Visa

D7 retirement visa

Same D7 visa as Porto and Lisbon — Portugal's standard retiree route. The 2026 income threshold is €920/month for a single applicant, plus 50% per dependent. The Algarve has a well-developed support infrastructure for the visa process; English-speaking immigration lawyers and accountants are easy to find in Lagos, Faro, and Tavira.

Healthcare

SNS + English-speaking private care

The Algarve's large expat community has created a robust network of English-speaking private GPs, dentists, and specialists — particularly around Lagos, Albufeira, and Faro. The public SNS Centro de Saúde is available to residents. Private health insurance for two adults runs €80–130/month per person at age 50–65. Faro Hospital handles major cases.

Taxes

NHR is gone — same story as Porto

Portugal's Non-Habitual Resident tax program closed fully in early 2025. Standard Portuguese income tax rates apply to residents. The successor IFICI program targets researchers and tech professionals, not retirees. Portugal still has a US tax treaty, and the overall tax environment remains competitive — but the old NHR deal is over. Get a cross-border CPA before making decisions.
