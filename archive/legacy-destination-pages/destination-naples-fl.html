Why People Choose Naples

The vibe in four cards

Naples has a reputation for being Florida's most refined retirement destination. That reputation is earned — but it also means you pay for it. Go in knowing both sides.

🏖️
The Gulf Coast's finest beaches
Naples beaches are consistently rated among Florida's best — powder-white sand, warm Gulf water, calm surf, spectacular sunsets facing west. Delnor-Wiggins Pass State Park, Clam Pass Beach, and Lowdermilk Park are all within 20 minutes. The beach isn't a trip here — it's Tuesday afternoon.
🍽️
Fifth Avenue South — a real dining scene
Naples has a restaurant scene that punches well above its size — Fifth Avenue South and Third Street South are lined with upscale dining, galleries, and boutiques that wouldn't look out of place in a major city. This isn't chain restaurant Florida. The food and social scene is genuinely good.
🏥
Healthcare at the highest level
NCH (Naples Comprehensive Health) is the local anchor — a respected regional system with two hospitals. Cleveland Clinic Florida is 45 minutes north in Weston. Mayo Clinic in Jacksonville is a day trip. For serious medical needs, Naples retirees have better access than most US cities of similar size. And Medicare works everywhere.
⛳
Golf, boating, and the outdoor life
Naples has more golf courses per capita than almost any US city — private clubs, semi-private, and public. The Ten Thousand Islands and Everglades are minutes away for kayaking and fishing. The lifestyle here is active and outdoors-oriented, not just beach sitting. If you want to fill your days, this city makes it easy.

Life on the Ground

A Wednesday evening in Naples

You live in a 2BR condo in North Naples — $2,400/mo rent, pool, 10 minutes from the beach. Your unit is well-maintained, the complex is quiet, and the HOA handles everything. You moved here from Cleveland two years ago and you cannot believe it took you this long.

Wednesday mornings you do yoga at a studio on Pine Ridge Road, then coffee with the group that's formed around the 8am class — six people, all within five years of your age, all of whom moved here from somewhere cold. Wednesday afternoons are beach time. You park at Lowdermilk and walk south. The water is 82°F. You swim for 45 minutes and then read in the sand until the light starts to change.

Tonight, dinner on Fifth Avenue South. A well-regarded spot with outdoor seating. Grouper, a good Sauvignon Blanc, crème brûlée — $95 for two with tip. It's a little more than you'd spend back home. The weather alone makes it feel like a good deal.

The one thing to be honest about: summer. June through September is hot, humid, and stormy. Some residents go north for those months. If you can't do that, understand what July in Naples feels like before you commit.

Before You Go

The practical stuff

Florida is genuinely favorable for retirees from a tax and logistics standpoint. A few real-world considerations are worth knowing.

💰
Florida's retirement tax picture
Florida has no state income tax — Social Security, pension income, and investment withdrawals are not taxed at the state level. Property taxes: Naples is in Collier County, which has relatively high property values but a homestead exemption that reduces assessed value for primary residents. No estate or inheritance tax in Florida. Overall, one of the most tax-favorable states for retirees in the US.
🌀
Hurricane season and insurance
Southwest Florida is in hurricane territory — Ian (2022) significantly impacted the region. Homeowners insurance and flood insurance are mandatory if you own, and have risen sharply post-Ian. Many condos and HOAs handle building insurance, but you'll need personal property and umbrella coverage. Renters have less to worry about structurally, but disruption during storms is real. Factor insurance costs into your budget; they've increased meaningfully in recent years.
🏘️
Neighborhoods: where matters a lot
Naples is not uniform. Old Naples (near downtown and Fifth Ave) is walkable and expensive. North Naples has newer development and more options for renters. East Naples is more affordable but requires a car for everything. Bonita Springs just north is a related market. Pelican Bay and Pelican Landing are master-planned communities with robust amenities. The neighborhood you choose shapes the daily experience substantially — worth researching before committing.
