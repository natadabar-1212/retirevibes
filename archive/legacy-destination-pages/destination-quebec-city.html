Why Québec City might be your place

Four reasons this came up at the top.

The most European city in North America — a walled city older than most American states, a world-famous Winter Carnival, a rich culinary scene, and costs well below Montréal or Toronto.

🏰

A UNESCO World Heritage city — the whole city

Vieux-Québec (Old Québec) is the only fortified city north of Mexico with its walls still intact. The Château Frontenac rises above the St. Lawrence like something from a dream. Narrow cobblestone streets, 400-year-old stone buildings, the Plains of Abraham. This is not a city with a historic district — the whole city is the historic district.

🇫🇷

France — without the flight

Québec City is 95% French-speaking, and French here is genuine — it predates France's own modern dialect in some respects. The cuisine is serious: cassoulet, poutine elevated to an art form, sugar shack culture in spring, a wine and cheese scene that rivals European capitals. For people drawn to French culture who aren't ready to navigate a European visa, Québec City is the practical answer.

❄️

A city that makes winter worth it

Québec City gets real winters — snowfall averages over 300cm annually, temperatures regularly dip to -20°C. But the city leans into it in a way few places do. The Winter Carnival (Carnaval de Québec) is the world's largest, running three weeks each February. The Old City transforms into something genuinely magical. If you've always wanted to love winter rather than endure it, this is where that happens.

💰

The most affordable walled UNESCO city on earth

A comparable lifestyle in Florence, Porto, or Prague would cost significantly more. Québec City offers European-scale culture, history, and culinary depth at Canadian prices — which, relative to Montréal or Toronto, are themselves reasonable. The CAD exchange rate (currently ~$0.73 USD/CAD) adds further advantage for Americans with USD income or savings.

What a Saturday in Québec City feels like.

Morning: coffee and a fresh croissant at a boulangerie on Rue Saint-Jean — one of the best bakeries in North America occupies a 300-year-old stone building, and there's a line out the door, and nobody seems annoyed by this. You eat slowly. The light comes through old glass at an angle that only happens in rooms this old.

Afternoon walk through Vieux-Québec — up the funicular to Terrasse Dufferin above the river, where the view across the St. Lawrence stretches to the mountains of the Laurentians. In February this terrace is an ice slide. In July it's packed with music festival crowds. Today it's quiet enough to see every detail of the river below. You walk the ramparts. The city walls are six meters thick in places.

Dinner in Saint-Roch — the Lower Town neighborhood that's been Québec City's creative engine for a decade. A chef's table at a bistro doing Québécois cuisine with serious technique. Wild game, foraged mushrooms, local cheeses. The sommelier speaks some English. You manage. The wine is excellent.

The practical stuff

Three things to know before you go further.

Residency

No retirement visa — Québec PNP is the path

Canada has no retirement visa. Québec controls its own immigration under the Canada-Québec Accord — the Québec Provincial Nominee Program (PNP) is separate from federal Express Entry. Most retirees without a skilled worker profile will find this challenging. The most realistic routes: family sponsorship, certain investor streams, or the 6-months-per-year visitor approach. A licensed immigration consultant (RCIC or immigration lawyer) is essential.

Language

French matters here — English is secondary

Québec City is more French-dominant than Montréal — the majority of daily life happens in French, and making an effort is both practically important and warmly received. Most service workers understand English, particularly in the tourist-facing parts of Old Québec. But administrative processes (healthcare registration, housing contracts, government services) are predominantly in French. Investing in French lessons before you arrive will pay off daily.

Taxes

Québec tax rates are among Canada's highest

Québec has separate provincial income tax on top of federal Canadian tax — combined effective rates for retirement income can be significant. The US-Canada tax treaty reduces double taxation, but US citizens resident in Canada must still file US taxes annually, report Canadian accounts under FBAR and FATCA, and navigate the interaction between Canadian RRSP/RRIF accounts and US tax treatment. A cross-border CPA is non-negotiable.
