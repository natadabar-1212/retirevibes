Where to spend your time

Burlington neighborhoods

Burlington is a compact city — you can walk from the lakefront to Church Street to the Hill in under 20 minutes. But the feel shifts meaningfully between districts. Give yourself at least two full days here before forming an opinion.

Church Street & Downtown

The walkable core — pedestrian mall, independent shops, the farmers' market on Saturday mornings, great coffee and restaurants in every direction. Start here to get a feel for Burlington's energy. Spend a weekday morning and a Saturday afternoon; the difference tells you a lot about who lives here versus who visits.

The Hill Section & South End

Where many long-term Burlington residents actually live — Victorian homes, quieter streets, easy walking distance to both Church Street and the waterfront. The South End arts district adds galleries, studios, and a Saturday market that rivals the downtown one. This is where you see Burlington beyond the tourist layer.

Waterfront & New North End

The waterfront path is Burlington at its most beautiful — particularly in fall foliage season and on summer evenings when the light on Lake Champlain turns gold. The New North End is more residential, family-oriented, and often overlooked — but it offers realistic housing prices and genuinely quiet streets close to the lake.

Planning a visit

When to scout Burlington

Best months to visit

September–October for peak fall foliage — arguably the best in New England. Late May and June when the lake is calm and the city wakes up from winter. If skiing matters to you, a January or February trip to see a full Vermont winter is essential.

Worth knowing

Vermont winters are serious — cold, snowy, and long. Burlington gets 80+ inches of snow annually. That's not a reason to rule it out, but it's a reason to experience one before committing. March and early April can feel especially drawn-out.

Talk to a retirement advisor

Vermont has no Social Security tax and moderate property taxes — but state income tax and high housing costs deserve a close look with a fiduciary who knows the region.
