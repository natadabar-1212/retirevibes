Chattanooga rewrote itself over two decades — from a Rust Belt river city to one of the most-cited downtown revivals in the South. The Tennessee River runs through it, Lookout Mountain rises above it, the Ocoee River whitewater is 30 minutes east, and Tennessee has no state income tax at all. The cost of living is among the lowest of any outdoor-focused US city worth living in.

Why People Choose Chattanooga

The vibe in four cards

Chattanooga surprises people — it's consistently underestimated until you're actually there. The river, the mountains, the food scene, and the tax environment create a combination that's hard to find anywhere else at this price.

🏔️
Lookout Mountain and the Cumberland Plateau
Lookout Mountain rises 2,392 feet directly above the city — trails, Civil War battlefields, the famous Rock City gardens, and Ruby Falls (a 145-foot underground waterfall) are all on its flanks. The Appalachian Trail is within driving distance. The Ocoee River — site of the 1996 Olympics whitewater events — is 30 minutes east and offers commercial rafting all summer. Cloudland Canyon State Park in Georgia is 45 minutes away. The outdoor radius here is legitimately exceptional for a city this size and this affordable.
🌊
The Tennessee Riverwalk — 13 miles through the city
The Tennessee Riverwalk follows both banks of the Tennessee River for 13 continuous miles, connecting Coolidge Park on the North Shore to the downtown waterfront to the Chickamauga Dam east of the city. It's the spine of outdoor life in Chattanooga — walkers, cyclists, kayakers, families. The North Shore neighborhood sits on one bank: a concentration of restaurants, coffee shops, and green space that represents the best of the city's revival. The Walnut Street Bridge, one of the longest pedestrian bridges in the world, connects the two sides.
🍽️
A food scene that kept winning awards
Chattanooga's restaurant scene is out of proportion to its size — a legacy of the talent and investment that accompanied the downtown revival. The Southside neighborhood, the North Shore, and the Market Street corridor all have concentrated dining. Farm-to-table Tennessee cooking alongside authentic immigrant cuisines (a large Congolese and Vietnamese community shapes the food landscape in real ways). The Main Street Farmers' Market runs April through November. James Beard nominations have followed the chefs who built careers here.
💰
Tennessee's tax environment
Tennessee has no state income tax — period. Social Security is not taxed. Pension income is not taxed. Retirement account withdrawals are not taxed at the state level. The state does have a sales tax (9.75% combined state/local — among the highest in the country), but for retirees drawing fixed income rather than earning wages, the net tax picture is highly favorable. Combined with some of the lowest housing costs of any mid-size city with genuine outdoor and cultural amenities, Chattanooga's financial case is unusually strong.

Life on the Ground

A Sunday on the river

You live in a 2BR cottage in the North Shore neighborhood — $1,600/month, front porch, two blocks from Coolidge Park and the Riverwalk. You moved from Columbus, Ohio two years ago. The mortgage-to-rent math didn't work in Columbus anymore. Here, it almost does.

Sunday morning: walk to the Riverwalk before 8am. The Tennessee River is glassy and wide and the mist is still on it. You walk 4 miles to the Walnut Street Bridge and back. A bald eagle is perched in a sycamore on the far bank. This happens often enough that you've stopped photographing it.

Brunch on the North Shore at a spot with biscuits that have been written about nationally. $22 for two, including coffee. Afternoon: drive up to Lookout Mountain. There's a trail along the battlefield ridge that you've been working through. The views of the river valley from the top, bending south into Georgia, are extraordinary.

Dinner: a newer spot in the Southside doing James Beard–quality Southern food without the reservation two months out. $65 for two with wine. Your neighbor, a retired teacher from Atlanta who moved here four years ago, texts to say she's at the bar. You walk over. She says she can't believe how well this city has worked out. You know exactly what she means.

Before You Go

The practical stuff

Tennessee is one of the simplest US states to retire in from a tax standpoint. Here's what else to know.

💰
Tennessee's retirement tax picture
Tennessee eliminated its Hall Income Tax completely in 2021 — the last vestige of state income taxation. There is now zero state income tax on wages, Social Security, pensions, 401(k)/IRA withdrawals, or investment income. The trade-off is Tennessee's combined sales tax of 9.75% (7% state + local), which is among the highest in the country. For retirees drawing fixed income rather than earning wages, the net picture is very favorable. Property taxes in Hamilton County (Chattanooga) are among the lowest for any comparable metro.
🏥
Healthcare: Erlanger and CHI Memorial
Erlanger Health System is the flagship — a 581-bed Level I Trauma Center affiliated with the University of Tennessee College of Medicine. It's the only Level I Trauma Center in the region, covering southeast Tennessee and northwest Georgia. CHI Memorial Hospital and Parkridge Medical Center offer additional capacity. For specialized complex care, Vanderbilt University Medical Center in Nashville is 2 hours west — one of the top academic medical centers in the Southeast. Medicare accepted at all facilities.
🏙️
Neighborhoods — location matters
Chattanooga's best retirement neighborhoods are highly location-specific. The North Shore offers the most walkable, vibrant residential environment — close to the Riverwalk, Coolidge Park, and the best restaurants. Downtown and the Southside are increasingly residential and walkable. Lookout Mountain (technically part in Georgia) is quieter, wooded, and residential — beautiful but requires a car for everything. East Brainerd and suburban areas east of the city are affordable but generic. The North Shore, particularly within a few blocks of the Riverwalk, is where most happy transplants end up.
