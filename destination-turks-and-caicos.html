The Vibe

The Caribbean at its
most refined.

Turks & Caicos is for people who want the Caribbean experience without compromise. Grace Bay Beach routinely tops global rankings — the water is an impossible turquoise, the sand is powdered sugar white, and the whole scene is better in person than in photos, which is rare.

This is an upscale destination and it knows it. Providenciales — "Provo" to everyone who lives here — has a genuine international community of retirees, remote workers, and people who decided the mainland commute wasn't worth it. The infrastructure is modern, English is the official language, and the USD is the official currency.

No income tax on the islands makes the financial picture cleaner than most international moves. There's no capital gains tax either. The trade-off is cost of living — this is one of the pricier Caribbean options, comparable to premium US coastal resort towns. But for the lifestyle on offer, many residents consider the calculus simple.

What You'll Love

Life in Turks & Caicos

🏖️

Grace Bay Beach

Consistently ranked the world's best beach. Twelve miles of uninterrupted white sand and water that shifts from turquoise to cobalt depending on depth. It's right outside your door — not a day trip.

🚫

No Income Tax

The Turks & Caicos Islands levy no income tax, no capital gains tax, and no inheritance tax. For retirees drawing on investment income or Social Security, the tax picture is worth a serious conversation with an advisor.

🤿

World-Class Diving

The third-largest coral reef system in the world runs along the islands. The Wall — a sheer drop from shallow reef to 6,000-foot depths — is one of the most dramatic dives in the hemisphere.

🍽️

International Dining

Provo has a restaurant scene that would hold its own in any major city. Fresh conch, Caribbean lobster, and international cuisine from a community of chefs who chose TCI deliberately.

💊

Modern Medical Care

Interhealth Canada Hospital and several private clinics handle most needs on Provo. For major procedures, Miami is a 90-minute flight. Medical evacuation insurance is standard practice.

🛥️

Boating & Water Sports

Kiteboarding at Long Bay, kayaking through mangrove channels, whale watching off Grand Turk in spring, and deep-sea fishing year-round. The water here is the destination.

Where to Land

Islands & Areas

Most expats settle on Providenciales, the main inhabited island. Each area has a distinct character.

Grace Bay

Most Sought-After

The strip along Grace Bay Beach is the heart of expat and tourist life — luxury condos, boutique hotels, top restaurants, and direct beach access. The highest concentration of amenities. Properties here command the highest prices on the island.

Long Bay Hills

Most Active

The kiteboarding capital of the Caribbean, with consistent winds and wide shallow water. More residential and local-feeling than Grace Bay. Popular with younger retirees and active water sports enthusiasts. Prices are somewhat lower.

Leeward

Most Private

A quieter residential area on the northeast tip of Provo — gated communities, deep-water marina access, and some of the most impressive private villas on the island. A short drive to Grace Bay, but with more space and less traffic.

Grand Turk

Most Historic

The small capital island — quieter, more authentically Caribbean, with a charming colonial main street and famous Wall diving. A different pace from Provo. Some retirees prefer its unhurried character and lower price point.

Know Before You Go

What to plan for

Residency & Belonger Status

The Annual Residency Permit (ARP) is the most common route for retirees — it requires proof of financial sufficiency and a clean background check, and runs around $1,500/year. Permanent Residency (PRP) is available after several years and requires a real estate investment or substantial income demonstration.

Cost of Goods

Nearly everything except seafood and sunshine is imported. Groceries run 30–50% above US mainland prices. Building a home costs significantly more. Many residents do a major Costco or Sam's Club run when traveling to the US. Factor the import premium into any budget planning.

Hurricane Exposure

TCI has been hit by significant hurricanes — Irma in 2017 caused major damage. The islands have rebuilt with upgraded codes. Insurance is available but expensive. Most permanent residents have a storm plan and strong wind coverage. Season runs June–November.

Healthcare Gaps

For day-to-day care, Provo's facilities are solid. For complex surgery, cardiac events, or cancer treatment, Miami or Nassau is the destination. Medical evacuation insurance (around $300–500/year) is considered essential, not optional.

US Tax Obligations

US citizens owe US taxes regardless of where they live — TCI's no-income-tax benefit applies to local taxes only. You still file with the IRS. The Foreign Earned Income Exclusion and Foreign Tax Credit don't apply the same way in a zero-tax jurisdiction. Talk to a cross-border tax advisor.

USD Currency

The US Dollar is the official currency of TCI — no exchange rate to manage, no currency conversion fees on USD purchases, and a straightforward banking relationship with US institutions. One of the simplest financial transitions of any international destination.
