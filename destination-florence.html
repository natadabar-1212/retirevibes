Why People Choose Florence

The vibe in four cards

💡
A note on cost: Florence is the most expensive European destination on our list — roughly $1,000–1,500/mo more than Porto or Athens. If the Renaissance art, the food culture, and the Tuscany lifestyle are the draw, that gap is the price of admission. Go in with eyes open.
🎨
The world's greatest art — on foot
The Uffizi holds Botticelli's Birth of Venus and thousands of other masterworks. The Accademia has Michelangelo's David. Palazzo Pitti, the Bargello, Santa Croce — there's more world-class art within walking distance of a typical Florence apartment than in most entire countries. As a resident, you stop treating it as a sightseeing checklist and start treating it as your neighborhood.
🍷
Tuscany is your backyard
The Chianti wine region starts 20 minutes south of the city. Siena is 75 minutes by bus. San Gimignano, Lucca, Volterra, the Cinque Terre — you live inside one of the most beautiful landscapes in the world, with a train and a driver's license that gets you anywhere on weekends. This is not a metaphor. It's a 20-minute drive.
🍝
The real Italian food
Ribollita. Bistecca alla Fiorentina. Fresh pasta made the morning you eat it. Lampredotto from a vendor near the Mercato Centrale. Florence doesn't do fusion or trend-chasing — it does its own food better than anyone else. A neighborhood trattoria dinner with wine: €35–50 for two. The real Florentines eat there too.
🏙️
Small enough to know, big enough to live
Florence has about 370,000 people — compact enough that you learn the neighborhoods and the faces at your market stall, big enough to have everything you need. The Oltrarno (south side of the Arno) is the expat sweet spot: artisan workshops, quieter piazzas, better rents, and the views of the city from Piazzale Michelangelo above.

Life on the Ground

A Saturday in the city of the Medici

You live in Oltrarno — the artisan neighborhood on the south side of the Arno. Your 2BR apartment costs €1,600/mo. From the window you can see the Ponte Vecchio. You've stopped taking photos of it. It's just the view.

Saturday mornings you walk to the Santo Spirito market. Seasonal produce, local cheese, a vendor who saves you the good prosciutto. You spend €25. On the way back you pick up a cornetto and a macchiato at the bar for €2.20, standing at the counter the way the Italians do.

Afternoons, you've booked a spot at a ceramics studio in the neighborhood — run by a Florentine craftsman whose family has been making majolica for four generations. You're not particularly talented. That's not the point. You're learning something real with your hands in a city that has always valued craft.

In the evening you drive 25 minutes to a winery in Greve in Chianti for a harvest dinner. Three courses, local wine, the hills going golden in the dusk. €45 for two. You're back home by 10pm. Tomorrow you're doing nothing, and that also feels right.

Before You Go

The practical stuff

Italy has a specific retirement visa — and income requirements that are higher than most. Here's what you need to know before you start the process.

📋
Italy's Elective Residency Visa
Italy's Elective Residency Visa (ERV) requires demonstrating approximately €31,000/year (~$33,000+) in passive income per person — meaning a couple needs roughly €62,000/yr combined. This is the highest income bar on our list. Income must come from sources outside Italy: pensions, Social Security, annuities, investment income. Applied for at an Italian consulate in the US. You cannot work in Italy on this visa. An Italian immigration attorney is highly recommended — the paperwork requirements are detailed.
🏥
Healthcare: solid private and public options
Italy's public healthcare (SSN) is available to legal residents — including ERV holders. Many expats supplement or use private care for faster access and English-speaking providers. Careggi University Hospital is Florence's major public hospital. Private clinics like Villanova and Torregalli are alternatives. US Medicare does not cover you in Italy. Private insurance runs €150–350/mo depending on age and coverage; some opt for the public system after registering as residents.
💰
Taxes: Flat Tax regime for new residents
Italy offers a €100,000/year flat tax on all foreign-sourced income for new tax residents who haven't lived in Italy in the past 10 years — a significant benefit for those with substantial foreign income. (There's also a €25,000 version for retirees in specific Southern Italian towns.) Italy and the US have a tax treaty. You still file US taxes. A cross-border tax advisor is essential here — the interaction between the flat tax, the US treaty, and FBAR requirements needs expert navigation. Budget €1,500–3,000/yr for this.

Not Sure Yet?

Places with a similar vibe

Culture, history, and European food at the center of daily life. If Florence resonates but the cost gives you pause, these have some of the same pull.

🇬🇷
Athens, Greece
Mediterranean Europe
Also ancient, also walkable, also one of the world's great cultural capitals. Athens costs roughly $800–1,200/mo less for a couple. The Acropolis is older than the Duomo.
🇵🇹
Porto, Portugal
Southern Europe
Walkable old city with serious culture, excellent food, and a fraction of Florence's cost. Less Renaissance grandeur — but equally livable, and easier on the visa requirements.
🇪🇸
Málaga, Spain
Mediterranean Europe
More sun, lower costs, easier visa path. Picasso's birthplace instead of Michelangelo's David — but still genuinely rich cultural and food scene in the heart of southern Europe.
