Alicante is Spain done right — a real city with beaches, tapas bars, a medieval castle, and a year-round social life. It has everything Barcelona has, for about half the price.

The vibe

Spain without the
premium price tag.

Alicante is the kind of Mediterranean city that makes you wonder why you ever lived anywhere else. Palm-lined esplanades, white-sand beaches, ancient castles on rocky bluffs, and a food culture built around fresh fish, local wine, and long afternoon meals.


The city sits on Spain's southeastern coast, in the autonomous community of Valencia. Temperatures rarely dip below 50°F in winter, and summers are long, warm, and reliably dry. Unlike many Spanish beach towns that go quiet in the off-season, Alicante is a real city of 330,000 people — there's always something happening, someone to meet, somewhere new to explore.


The expat community here is one of the largest in Spain — mostly British, but growing numbers of Germans, Dutch, and increasingly Americans who discovered that the Non-Lucrative Visa makes retiring here genuinely straightforward. The TRAM (light rail) connects Alicante to surrounding beach towns, and the high-speed train gets you to Madrid in just over 2 hours.


Compared to Barcelona or Madrid, Alicante is significantly less expensive. Compared to Portugal's Algarve, it has more authentic city culture and deeper roots. For people who want Mediterranean Europe — the light, the food, the pace, the warmth — Alicante delivers it at a price that's hard to beat anywhere on the continent.

Where to live

City, coast, or somewhere in between.

Alicante's neighborhoods each offer a different slice of the Mediterranean life. Most expats try the center first, then migrate outward.

Historic center
El Centro / El Barrio

The old town at the foot of Castillo de Santa Bárbara. Narrow streets, tapas bars, weekend markets, and the famous Explanada de España palm promenade running along the port. Walkable to everything.

Beach living
Playa de San Juan

A 6km stretch of fine sand beach about 8km from the city center. Residential, quieter in winter, buzzing in summer. Lower rents than the center, with direct tram access to the city. Expat favorite.

Village & coast
El Campello

A charming fishing village 10km north of Alicante, still connected by the TRAM. Slower pace, beautiful small beach, genuine local restaurants. A favorite for those who want village life without giving up city access.

Day-to-day life

Long lunches and golden afternoons.

🍷
The food & drink culture

Alicante is in the heart of one of Spain's great wine and food regions. Paella originated nearby in Valencia. Arroz a banda (rice in fish broth) is a local specialty. The central Mercado Central overflows with fresh produce, seafood, and jamón. Tapas culture means it's normal to eat late, eat well, and eat socially.

🏖️
The beach lifestyle

Within city limits, Postiguet beach puts you in the Mediterranean in under 20 minutes from most apartments. Playa de San Juan stretches north with less crowd. The sea is warm enough to swim from May through October, and year-round swimming clubs keep the hardcore in the water all winter.

🏥
Healthcare

Spain's public healthcare system is among Europe's best. Non-lucrative visa holders need private insurance (required for the visa), typically €100–200/month. Private hospitals and clinics in Alicante are modern and affordable by US standards. Many American expats report receiving better care here than in the US.

✈️
Getting around & away

Alicante-Elche Airport has direct connections across Europe and to US hubs via Madrid or London. Within Spain, the Renfe high-speed train connects to Madrid in 2.5 hours. The TRAM connects Alicante to beach towns in both directions. Renting a car opens up the spectacular inland province.

The practical side

What you need to know.

Visa
Non-Lucrative Visa

Spain's Non-Lucrative Visa (Visado de Residencia No Lucrativa) is designed for retirees. Requires proof of passive income (~$2,600+/month) and private health insurance. Renewable annually, leads to permanent residency after 5 years.

Taxes
Spanish Income Tax

Spain taxes worldwide income for residents. The Beckham Law can offer a flat 24% rate for the first 6 years for qualifying newcomers. US-Spain tax treaty helps prevent double taxation. Work with a gestor (local tax advisor) from day one.

Banking
Easy

Major Spanish banks (Santander, BBVA, CaixaBank) are expat-friendly. You'll need your NIE (foreigner ID number) to open an account. Wise or Revolut work well as interim solutions while setting up residency.

Language
Spanish Helps a Lot

English is common in expat areas and tourist zones, but daily life is much richer if you speak Spanish. Alicante has a thriving expat social scene that can make it easy to avoid learning — but most who stay long-term say learning Spanish was the best thing they did.
