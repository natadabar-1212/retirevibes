🇻🇳 Da Nang, Vietnam

Beach city, Marble Mountains,
and Hoi An thirty minutes south

Da Nang is Vietnam's third-largest city — a modern beach destination on the South China Sea, with the Marble Mountains rising from the coast, the Hai Van Pass to the north, and Hoi An's UNESCO ancient town a 30-minute drive south. The cost of living is among the lowest of any cosmopolitan destination in the world. The infrastructure for expats has developed significantly in the last decade.

Why People Choose Da Nang

The vibe in four cards

Da Nang occupies a specific niche: a modern, functional beach city in a country with extraordinarily low costs, excellent food, and a geography that puts multiple UNESCO World Heritage sites within easy reach. It's not the most charming city in Vietnam — Hoi An holds that title — but it's the most livable for long-term residents.

🏖️
My Khe Beach — and the coast
Da Nang's My Khe Beach runs 30 kilometers along the South China Sea — a wide, clean, uncrowded stretch of sand that Forbes once listed among the most beautiful beaches in the world. The water is warm (80–84°F) from March through October; the beach is swimmable for much of the year. The Dragon Bridge, Da Nang's most famous landmark, breathes fire and water on weekend nights. North of the city, the Hai Van Pass — a mountain coastal road crossing a dramatic headland — is one of the most scenic drives in Southeast Asia, made famous by Top Gear. The combination of functioning beach town and extraordinary natural landscape within minutes of a modern city is Da Nang's primary geographical gift.
🏮
Hoi An — UNESCO ancient town 30 minutes away
Hoi An's Ancient Town is a UNESCO World Heritage Site — a remarkably preserved 15th–19th century trading port on the Thu Bon River, with Chinese merchant houses, Japanese covered bridge, Vietnamese temples, and French colonial buildings all within a walkable historic core. The lantern-lit old town at night is one of the most photographed scenes in Southeast Asia. For Da Nang residents, Hoi An is a 30-minute drive south — accessible for weekends, evening dinners, and whenever the mood for cobblestone streets and lantern light strikes. Many expats split their time: the modern conveniences and beach life of Da Nang for daily life, Hoi An for culture and atmosphere. The combination is available in very few places in the world.
🍜
Central Vietnamese cuisine
Da Nang sits in the heart of Central Vietnam, home to some of the country's most distinctive regional cooking. Mì Quảng (turmeric-stained noodles with shrimp, pork, and peanuts, minimal broth) is the Da Nang signature dish. Bánh mì is everywhere and excellent. Bún bò Huế (spicy lemongrass beef noodle soup from Hue, 1 hour north) is available at hundreds of street stalls. Cao lầu — a Hoi An specialty with thick noodles, pork, and herbs that can only be made with water from Hoi An's ancient wells — is 30 minutes away. A street food meal costs $1–3. A full dinner for two at a nice restaurant rarely exceeds $18. For people who consider food culture a primary factor in where to live, Central Vietnam's cuisine is seriously compelling.
💰
The lowest costs of any cosmopolitan destination
Da Nang is consistently the lowest-cost destination of any major RetireVibes match — for a reason. A 2BR apartment rents for $400–700/month. A full breakfast costs $1.50. A bowl of mì Quảng at a local stall is $1. An Uber or Grab (Southeast Asia's equivalent) across the city is $2–4. Private healthcare at Bangkok Hospital Da Nang, designed specifically for expats, charges $50–80 for a GP consultation. For anyone who has been told they can't afford to retire — or who genuinely wants to live on a Social Security income alone — Da Nang is one of the very few places where that calculation might actually work. These are facts about a place, not a promise about your finances. The math is yours to do.

Life on the Ground

A Thursday on the South China Sea

You rent a 2BR apartment in the My Khe Beach neighborhood — $580/month, ninth floor, a balcony facing east with a direct view of the South China Sea. You moved from Phoenix. In Phoenix, $580/month was roughly your electric bill. Here it's your apartment, two blocks from the beach.

Thursday morning: at My Khe by 6:30am — this is the best time, before the heat peaks and before the beach fills. You swim for 30 minutes. The water is 82°F. There are local families doing their morning exercise on the beach, vendors setting up, an older Vietnamese man doing tai chi in the shallow water. You have become a person who swims in the ocean on a Thursday morning. You didn't expect this.

Breakfast after: bánh mì from the stall two blocks from your building — pâté, pickled vegetables, jalapeño, coriander, the bread baked this morning. $1.20. Coffee at a small shop where the owner makes Vietnamese iced coffee — condensed milk, strong drip, ice — $1. This is breakfast for $2.20 and it is excellent.

Afternoon: drive to Hoi An. You've been 30 times and you still go. The Ancient Town in the late afternoon, before the tourist evening rush — the light on the yellow plaster walls, the wooden shophouses, the Thu Bon River. Dinner at a restaurant the locals use: white rose dumplings, cao lầu, grilled eggplant with lemongrass. $14 for two. Home to Da Nang by 9pm. Thursday.

Before You Go

The practical stuff

Vietnam's visa situation and property ownership rules are different from most countries — understanding them before you plan is essential.

🛂
Visas — e-visa and longer-term options
Vietnam's e-visa allows US citizens 90 days of stay, multiple entry, and can be renewed or extended with short border runs (typically to Thailand or Cambodia). For longer-term residence, Vietnam offers DT (Temporary Residence Card) visas, typically sponsored by a company, property ownership, or a Vietnamese spouse — none of which are straightforward for retirees. Vietnam does not have a dedicated retirement visa analogous to Thailand's, Mexico's, or Colombia's programs. The practical reality for many expats is extended stays on e-visas with periodic renewals or border runs, which is legally permissible but less settled than having a formal retirement residency status. This is the most significant practical limitation of Vietnam as a retirement destination — the visa infrastructure hasn't caught up to the expat interest. Consult an immigration attorney about current options, as the rules have evolved and continue to change.
🏥
Healthcare in Da Nang
Da Nang's expat healthcare infrastructure has improved significantly. Family Medical Practice Da Nang is a well-regarded international clinic designed for expat care — English-speaking doctors, international standards, GP consultations run $50–80. Bangkok Hospital Da Nang (affiliated with Bangkok Dusit Medical Services, Thailand's largest hospital group) opened in 2016 and handles more complex specialist care and minor surgical procedures. For major surgery, complex cardiac care, serious oncology, or highly specialized procedures, Bangkok (2.5 hours by direct flight) or Hanoi are significantly better options. International health insurance with medical evacuation coverage is strongly recommended — not just for Da Nang but for all of Vietnam. Many expats here also travel to Bangkok for regular specialist appointments. US Medicare does not apply outside the US.
🏠
Property ownership — understand the rules
Vietnam's 2014 Housing Law allows foreigners to own apartments (condominiums) under a 50-year leasehold title, renewable upon expiry — not freehold ownership. Foreigners cannot own land directly. In practice, most expats who buy in Da Nang purchase condominium apartments in designated projects, which provides secure ownership for the leasehold period. The 50-year term is renewable (subject to Vietnamese law at the time of renewal), but carries more uncertainty than freehold ownership in most other countries. Foreign ownership in any single development is capped at 30% of units. The legal landscape is evolving; a Vietnamese real estate attorney specializing in foreign transactions is essential before any purchase. Many long-term expats rent rather than buy — at $400–700/month for a 2BR, the financial case for buying isn't always obvious.

Places with a similar vibe

Penang, Malaysia
Southeast Asia
Also Southeast Asian, also UNESCO heritage city (George Town), also excellent food culture, similar costs. English is official language in Malaysia — less language adjustment than Vietnam. Better developed expat visa infrastructure. Different cultural flavor — Malaysian Chinese/Indian/Malay rather than Vietnamese.
🇹🇭
Chiang Mai, Thailand
Southeast Asia
Thailand has a more established expat infrastructure and a dedicated Retirement Visa (Non-O-A) that Da Nang currently lacks. Chiang Mai is mountains rather than beach, with similar costs. Better long-term residency stability for those who need it.
🇲🇽

Latin America
For those drawn to Da Nang's beach city lifestyle but who prefer staying in the Western Hemisphere — Playa del Carmen offers Caribbean coast, accessible Mexico Residente Temporal visa, more direct US flight access, and similar resort-adjacent urban energy at higher costs.
