Why San Miguel might be your place

Four reasons this came up at the top.

A UNESCO World Heritage city that happens to also be one of the most American-friendly places in Latin America — a genuinely soft landing into something genuinely different.

☁️

Eternal spring, no humidity

At 6,200 feet, San Miguel escapes the brutal heat of lowland Mexico. Days are warm and sunny — typically 70s — and evenings are cool enough for a sweater. The rainy season (June–September) brings afternoon showers, not monsoons. It's the rare place that's warm year-round without ever being oppressive.

🎨

Mexico's art capital

Dozens of galleries, working studios, a world-famous artists' residency program, and a Day of the Dead celebration that fills the streets with marigolds and music for a week. This isn't "arts-friendly" — it's a city where making things is the whole point. Writers, painters, sculptors, and photographers have been making San Miguel home since the 1940s.

🇺🇸

The softest soft landing in Latin America

About 14,000 Americans live here full-time — enough that your doctor probably speaks English, the bookshop carries English-language titles, and there's a thriving social scene from day one. The expat community can feel like a crutch or a lifeline, depending on where you are in the process. Either way, it's there when you need it.

✈️

Close enough to make it work

San Miguel sits 3.5 hours from the US border, with a major airport in Guanajuato (BJX) 90 minutes away. Direct flights connect to Houston, Dallas, Chicago, Los Angeles, and other US hubs. You're not on the other side of the world — you're a $200 round-trip from seeing your family. That changes the whole emotional calculation.

What a Sunday in San Miguel feels like.

Church bells wake you — not an alarm, just the Parroquia doing what it's done for 300 years. You make coffee, open the windows onto the courtyard, and bougainvillea spills over the wall in the morning light. The cobblestones outside are still wet from last night's rain.

Sunday market in the jardín — fresh tortillas, local cheese, herbs you don't have a name for yet. By noon you've run into three people you know from Spanish class and agreed to lunch at a place with a rooftop where you can see the whole valley. The food is excellent. The bill is $14.

Late afternoon: a gallery opening on Calle Umaran, then mezcal at a bar where the owner plays records and doesn't rush anyone. You walk home through streets that are lit like a postcard and feel, maybe for the first time in a long time, like you're exactly where you're supposed to be.

The practical stuff

Three things to know before you go further.

Visa

Residente Temporal → Permanente

Most Americans enter on a tourist permit (180 days), then convert to a Residente Temporal visa (1–4 years, renewable). The 2026 income threshold is approximately $4,200/month or $70,000 in savings. After 4 years as a Temporary Resident, you can apply for Permanent Residency. The process is handled through your nearest Mexican consulate before you arrive.

Healthcare

Private hospitals + IMSS option

San Miguel has three private hospitals — Hospital MAC, UNIMED, and Hospital Joya — with bilingual doctors trained in the US and Mexico. GP consultations run $30–50, specialists $50–100. Many expats enroll in Mexico's public IMSS system (~$600–900/year) as a low-cost safety net, paired with private insurance for elective and specialist care.

Taxes

US-Mexico treaty — still get a CPA

The US and Mexico have a tax treaty that prevents double taxation, but you'll still owe US taxes on your worldwide income as an American citizen abroad. Mexico taxes residents on Mexico-source income. The interaction between the two systems — especially for pension income, Social Security, and investment withdrawals — needs a cross-border CPA to sort out cleanly.
