Why People Choose Panama City

The vibe in four cards

Panama City is a real city — not a beach town or an expat enclave. It's a modern, connected, surprisingly cosmopolitan capital with one of the best retirement setups in the world.

💵
No exchange-rate math — ever
Panama officially uses the US dollar. Your Social Security, your pension, your investment withdrawals — they arrive in the same currency you spend. No currency risk, no conversion fees, no watching exchange rates. For most American retirees, this is a quiet but enormous quality-of-life win.
🏆
The Pensionado visa
Widely regarded as the best retirement visa in the Americas. Qualify with just $1,000/mo in guaranteed income (Social Security counts). Unlock 20–25% discounts on utilities, medications, airline tickets, restaurants, and entertainment. Permanent residency granted on approval — not a temporary status you keep renewing.
🏥
Johns Hopkins-affiliated care
Hospital Punta Pacífica carries a Johns Hopkins International affiliation — one of very few hospitals in Latin America with that credential. Hospital Nacional and Centro Médico Paitilla round out a strong private healthcare system. English-speaking doctors are common. A specialist visit: $60–90.
✈️
Three hours from Miami
Tocumen International Airport is one of Latin America's best hubs. Direct flights to Miami, Houston, New York, Atlanta, and 20+ other US cities. You're not disappearing into the jungle — you're moving somewhere you can get back from quickly, with a layover-free flight to most of the eastern US.

Life on the Ground

A Sunday in the city

You wake up in your apartment in Punta Pacífica — 29th floor, Pacific Ocean visible from the balcony, $1,800/mo. The city skyline across the bay looks like a smaller Miami. The canal is 20 minutes away.

Sunday mornings you drive to Casco Viejo, Panama City's UNESCO-listed historic district. The streets are colonial Spanish architecture draped in bougainvillea — now filled with craft coffee shops, galleries, and rooftop bars. Breakfast at a café: $9 for two. You walk the Amador Causeway afterward, watching cargo ships queue for the canal.

The Biomuseo in the afternoon — the Frank Gehry-designed natural history museum is a 20-minute walk away. Dinner somewhere in Marbella: good steak, glass of wine, $45 for two. Your neighbor is a retired nurse from Chicago who's been here six years. She tells you the Pensionado discounts alone save her $200/mo.

The heat is real — 85–90°F year-round, humid. But the apartments are well air-conditioned, the city is spread out, and most expats settle into it within a month. The tradeoff for everything else is easy to make.

Before You Go

The practical stuff

Panama has one of the most straightforward retirement paths in the world — but there are still a few things to understand before you start packing.

📋
The Pensionado visa — how it works
You need $1,000/mo in guaranteed income from a pension, Social Security, or annuity. (A couple needs only $1,000 total — not per person.) Submit documents through a Panamanian attorney: birth certificate, police record, proof of income, health certificate. Processing takes 3–6 months. Once approved, it's permanent residency — not an annual renewal. The Pensionado discounts (20–25% off utilities, meds, flights, hotels, restaurants) kick in immediately on a provisional card.
🏥
Healthcare: strong private system
US Medicare does not cover you in Panama. Most expats carry international health insurance ($200–400/mo depending on age and coverage) plus the Johns Hopkins-affiliated Hospital Punta Pacífica as their anchor. Specialist visits are $60–90. Dental and vision are a fraction of US costs. The Pensionado discount also applies to medications — typically 20% off at pharmacies.
💰
Taxes: very favorable for US retirees
Panama does not tax foreign-sourced income — meaning your SS, pension, and US investment income are not taxed by Panama. There is no US-Panama tax treaty, so you still file and pay US taxes as normal. Most expats engage a US expat CPA to ensure correct FBAR and FATCA filings. Budget ~$500–1,000/yr for that. No inheritance tax in Panama for close family.
